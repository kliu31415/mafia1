==================================================================================================
PART 1: THE GAME
==================================================================================================

R1 (Overview and winning)
You are playing a Mafia-style game with around 10 players (depending on setup), each in a faction. An omniscient
moderator runs the game. The game alternates Day and Night phases, starting with Day 1.
- For most roles, you win iff your faction wins. A faction typically wins iff it has players alive at the end.
- The game ends as soon as all living players, ignoring Jesters and Executioners, belong to one faction. This is
  checked after every death; deaths that happen together at night are checked together.
- Jesters and Executioners never block another faction's win; their own wins don't depend on how the game ends.

R2 (Factions)
- Village (Good): the largest faction, but with weaker roles and less information.
- Yakuza (Evil): fewer players, stronger roles; they know each other and share a night chat.
- Neutral: each Neutral player is their own one-person faction, even if two players have the same Neutral role.
The list of roles in the game is not public. A role may appear more than once in a game unless its text says
Unique.

R15 (Alignments)
Every player has one of three alignments: Good (Village), Evil (Yakuza) or Neutral. Some roles and effects change
how a player's alignment appears; see R19.

R18 (Terms)
- Living players: players who haven't died or left. Every count in these rules ("majority", "random player")
  means living players unless it says otherwise.
- Majority: strictly more than half; exactly half is not a majority.
- Action: anything a role does at night by choice or by its rules (investigating, shooting, blocking, ...).
- Visit: an action on another player counts as A visiting B, unless it is an Astral Visit (R6).
- Astral Visit: an action that is not a visit. Abilities that react to visits (Tracker, Lookout, Trapper,
  Fortune Teller) never see or react to it.
- Alignment check: a result that reports alignment (Sheriff, Seer). See R19 for what they see.
- Exact-role check: a result that reports or tests an exact role (Oracle, Diviner, Ritualist, Mortician).
- Token: a limited-use resource a role starts with. Spent tokens are gone; a role-blocked action spends none.
- Attack/Defense levels, lowest to highest: None, Basic, Standard, Strong, Divine (R3).

R11 (The circle)
Players sit in a circle. The dead (and players who leave) are removed from it.

==================================================================================================
PART 2: THE DAY
==================================================================================================

R4 (Day phase, chat, nominations and trials)
Chat:
- There is a public chat and private chats between exactly two players.
- Private messages are not secret: whenever A sends B a private message, everyone sees that A sent B a private
  message, but only A and B see its contents.
Nominations:
- Any player may publicly nominate someone, including themself. All chat stops at once.
- Everyone votes Yes/No at the same time; nobody sees another vote before casting their own. The nominator votes
  Yes by default. There are no abstentions: a player who doesn't vote counts as voting No. Once all votes are in,
  every vote is revealed.
- If a majority of all votes counted are Yes, the nominee goes on trial.
Trials:
- The nominee says the first public message and may defend themself; others may chat publicly and privately.
  After everyone else has had a turn, the nominee gets one last public message before the vote.
- Then everyone votes Guilty/Innocent at the same time (no abstentions: a player who doesn't vote counts as voting
  Innocent); every vote is revealed.
- If a majority of all votes counted are Guilty, the nominee is Executed by Hanging, a Divine Attack, and the Day
  ends immediately.
- If three nominations pass (failed ones don't count) and all three trials end Innocent, the Day ends.
- Some roles can change how votes are counted. If an announced vote total doesn't match the revealed votes,
  a role manipulated the count on purpose; it is not an error. One use of such a role can change every trial
  that Day, so the number of changed totals doesn't show how many times a role was used.
Voting Yes or Guilty on yourself is allowed.

R16 (Day length)
Day chat goes around the circle in turns. Each Day the program picks a random living player to speak first;
turns then go around the circle from them in the same order every round. Besides the endings in R4, the Day ends when a full round passes in
which nobody sends a public message or nominates, except that anyone sent a private message during that round
first gets one last turn. The Day also ends after at most 8 rounds.

R22 (Speaking budget)
Day messages and nominations cost speaking budget. Each player earns budget every round of a Day, up to a cap,
and part of what they have left carries into the next Day. A message's price grows with its length and, after
the first rounds of a Day, with the round; private messages cost less than public ones. A nomination costs
budget too. On a trial turn prices are lower and each speaker also gets a trial-only budget, spent first and lost
when the turn ends; the accused's first defense message is free up to a set length. The program sets the
numbers for each game and tells every player at the start.

R23 (Swaps)
Some roles can swap two players for a night. Every action that night aimed at one of them (including the Katana's
attack and automatic actions such as the Ex-Con's visit or the Seer's pair) lands on the other instead, as if its
user had chosen that player. Only the swap itself is not moved (the swapping player's Katana vote is). Results name the player the action's
user chose, so a swapped action's result doesn't show the swap: a Sheriff who checked A learns B's alignment as
A's. A Lookout who watched A watches B instead, and a Tracker sees the player its target's action landed on.

R13 (Last wills)
Players may write and update a last will at the beginning of each Day (except those who died the night before)
and during any of their own Day turns. When a player dies, their last will is shared publicly at once.

==================================================================================================
PART 3: THE NIGHT
==================================================================================================

R5 (Night phase)
Chat is off for most players; some (usually a faction, like the Yakuza) have a private night chat. The moderator
wakes players one by one, or faction by faction, to act. When the Night ends, the moderator works out every
effect. By default, effects are announced publicly at the start of the next Day; some are not announced.

R7 (Optional actions)
Every ability is optional ("may") unless its role says "must": any player may choose not to act at Night, not to
use a Day ability, and not to spend a token. Effects a role has automatically, with no choice (e.g. the
Ex-Con's nightly visit), aren't optional, but a role-block still stops them (R9).

R6 (Visits)
- When A performs an action on B, A visits B, unless the action is an Astral Visit.
- A role that targets a player may target itself unless its text says otherwise (e.g. "other than yourself").
- A player whose role can target others but who targets themself shows as visiting themself.
- A player whose action can only affect themself does not show as visiting anyone.
- An action that targets no player (e.g. the Clerk's rig) is not a visit.

R9 (Role-blocks)
A role-blocked player's actions that night have no effect, exactly as if they had chosen not to act: no visit,
no tokens spent. It lasts only that one night (not the next Day or later nights). Role-blocked players are told
they were role-blocked. Any player can be role-blocked unless their role says otherwise; blocking a player who
has no night action, or who chose not to act, has no other effect, but they are still told. A role-blocker learns
only whether the block failed because its target can't be role-blocked.

R10 (Simultaneous night resolution)
All night actions happen at the same time. Think of it as two steps: first everyone attempts their actions,
then all results happen together. So investigations that give their result at the end of the night (e.g. the
Seer's random pair) draw from and report on everyone alive when the night began, including players who die that
night.

R21 (Effects that outlast their user)
An effect set up at night for the following Day (e.g. a Clerk's rig, a Prosecutor's prosecution) still happens if
its user dies that night, unless it needs its user to act during that Day (e.g. a Puppeteer writing the puppet's
messages, a Ballot Stuffer's doubled vote); those lapse. Any token spent on it is gone either way (R18).

R20 (Night resolution checklist): a summary of the rules above, in the order the moderator applies them.
1. Remove actions of role-blocked players (R9), automatic ones included (the Ex-Con's visit, the Seer's pair).
   Party Animal, Pharmacist, Smuggler and Veteran can't be role-blocked. Swaps (R23) apply before anything else.
2. Work out who visits whom (R6), including the Katana holder's visit (R8) and the Ex-Con's random visit.
3. Attacks: each kills iff its level beats the target's Defense (R3). Defenses don't stack; the highest counts.
   Traps hit non-Village visitors (Trapper).
4. Results for investigators, using the visits and appearances (R19).
5. Notifications: attackers learn whether they succeeded, attacked players learn the attacking role (R3, with the
   Katana exception in R8), protectors learn nothing (R17), role-blocked players are told (R9), Yakuza learn each
   other's actions (R8).
6. Deaths are revealed at the start of the Day (R12); the win check runs on all of the night's deaths together (R1).

==================================================================================================
PART 4: ATTACKS, DEATH AND INFORMATION
==================================================================================================

R3 (Attack and Defense)
- Levels, lowest to highest: None, Basic, Standard, Strong, Divine. Players have Defense None by default.
- An Attack kills iff its level is strictly higher than the target's Defense.
- Defenses don't stack: a player with several sources of Defense has the highest of them.
- By default the attacker learns whether the Attack succeeded (not the target's Defense), and the target learns
  the role that attacked them (not the attacker's name or Attack level). The Katana is an exception (R8).

R17 (Protection feedback)
Protectors (e.g. the Medic) are never told whether their protection stopped an attack, and a protected player is
never told who protected them. A player who is attacked is still told as in R3.

R12 (Death reveals)
When someone dies, their role, cause of death (e.g. Katana, Vigilante, Execution by Hanging) and last will are
revealed publicly by default. If several attacks were each fatal, every fatal cause is revealed; attacks the
player survived aren't. They leave the circle and can't take part in the game any more.

R14 (Stall rule)
If a Day begins and nobody has died in the three previous Nights and three previous Days, everyone loses. Deaths
from last Night count even though they're announced today. Every death counts; leaving (like the Executioner's)
doesn't.

R19 (What investigations see)
Alignment checks (Sheriff: Evil / Not Evil; Seer: compares Good / Neutral / Evil) see a player's true alignment,
except:
- Yakuza holding the Katana at the end of the night: Good (Sheriff: Not Evil), even if they died that night.
- Player framed by the Fabricator that night: Evil.
- Ex-Con: Evil.
- Sheriff on Neutrals: Jester and Executioner read Evil; Serial Killer and Arsonist read Not Evil. (Seers see
  every Neutral as Neutral.)
The Census Taker's count is not an alignment check: it always sees true alignment, so none of the exceptions
above apply to it.
Exact-role checks see the true role. The Roleplayer is seen as "Roleplayer".
Token counts (Accountant) see the true total at the end of the night. The Roleplayer is seen as having 0 tokens,
even though they believe they have their fake role's tokens.
Visit-based results (Tracker, Lookout, Fortune Teller) are never changed by any of these.

==================================================================================================
PART 5: THE YAKUZA
==================================================================================================

R8 (Yakuza and the Katana)
- One random Yakuza starts with the Katana. The Yakuza share a private chat at night.
- Each night the Yakuza vote, at the same time, on who holds the Katana that night (and the next Day) and whom it
  attacks, if anyone. Both choices need a majority of living Yakuza; with two Yakuza alive, both must agree.
  Without a majority on a target, no attack happens; without a majority on a holder, the Katana doesn't move.
  A majority may move the Katana without attacking.
- The Katana attack is Standard and is dealt by whoever holds it at the end of the night. It is an action and a
  visit by that player (the new holder, if it moved that night): role-blocking them stops it, and Trackers see
  them visit the target. Passing the Katana is not a visit.
- The holder may also use their own role's night action.
- The holder appears Good to alignment checks that night (R19).
- The attacked player only learns they were attacked with the Katana, not the Yakuza role.
- At the end of the night, each Yakuza learns what actions the other Yakuza chose, but not their results
  (e.g. whether the Pharmacist's block worked) or whether a teammate was role-blocked.
- Role-blocked Yakuza still vote on the Katana's holder and target; the votes aren't actions.
- If the holder dies, the Katana passes to a random living Yakuza, if any; a holder who dies at night passes it on
  after that night's results. The living Yakuza are told who holds it now (and so learn that the holder died).

==================================================================================================
PART 6: ROLES
==================================================================================================

Every role lists the same fields. Defaults, unless a role says otherwise: Defense None, no tokens, appears as its
true alignment (R19), night action is a visit.

---------------------------------------- Village (Good) ----------------------------------------

Townsfolk (most villagers, helpful to the Village)

Vanilla Villager
  Night: none.

Investigative

Sheriff
  Night: may investigate one player. Learn Evil or Not Evil (alignment check, R19).

Tracker
  Night: may track one player. Learn whom they visited.

Lookout
  Night: may watch one player. Learn who visited them, not counting your own visit.

Seer
  Tokens: 1 seer token.
  Night: at the end of every night, you are told two random players other than yourself who were alive when the
  night began (players who die that night count, R10), and whether their alignments (Good/Neutral/Evil) are the
  same (alignment check, R19). Instead, you may spend a token to choose the
  two living players yourself (not including yourself). A chosen pair is a visit to both players; the random
  pair isn't a visit. A role-block stops both.

Fortune Teller
  Day: at the start of each Day except the first, you get two numbers without being told which is which: how many
  players were visited last night (a player visited several times counts once), and how many players visited
  anyone last night (a player who visited several players counts once). Self-visits (R6) count in both numbers.
  Example: A holds the Katana, attacks B and also uses their own role's action on C, D visits B, and E protects
  themself. Visited: B, C and E, so 3. Visitors: A, D and E, so 3. Equal numbers don't mean nobody was visited
  twice.

Oracle
  Night: may guess one player's exact role (Astral Visit). At the end of the night, learn whether you were right.

Eavesdropper
  Night: may eavesdrop on one player. Only during the following Day (not the Night you eavesdrop), you read the contents of every private
  message they send or receive, as it is sent. Puppeted messages reach you as the puppet's (you aren't told about
  the puppeting). They aren't told.

Archivist
  Tokens: 1 archive token.
  Night: may spend the token to consult the archive. At the end of the night, let N be the number of players other
  than you who were alive when the night began (players who died that night count): you are shown the roles of floor(0.75 * N) of them, chosen at random, in random order and
  without names. A role appears as many times as it was drawn, so two drawn Vanilla Villagers show twice. This
  is an exact-role check (R19): a Roleplayer shows as Roleplayer.

Surveyor
  Tokens: 2 survey tokens.
  Night: may spend a token to survey one player other than yourself. The group is that player plus their nearest
  neighbor on each side in the circle (R11) among the players alive at the start of the night, skipping yourself
  and never counting anyone twice (so with only one or two other players alive, the group is smaller). You are
  told who was in the group and the exact role of one of them chosen at random, but not whose it is. Players who
  die that night can still be chosen. This is an exact-role check (R19): a Roleplayer shows as Roleplayer.

Census Taker
  Night: may take a census around one player other than yourself (a visit to that player only).
  The program adds two other players drawn at random from everyone alive at the start of the night except you
  (players who die that night can be drawn; if fewer than two others are left, the group is smaller). It also
  draws at random whether to count Good or Evil players. You are told who was in the group, which alignment was
  counted, and how many of the group are of it. It counts true alignment and is not an alignment check (R19):
  the Katana holder, framed players and the Ex-Con count as what they really are. Neutral players are never
  counted, as either Good or Evil.

Accountant
  Night: may audit one player. Learn how many tokens they have in total at the end of the night, after any they
  spent that night (token count, R19). You learn only the total, not what kind of tokens they are.
  If their role changed at the end of this night, the role just before the change counts.

Combat

Medic
  Tokens: 2 medic tokens.
  Night: may protect one player other than yourself, giving them Standard Defense. Spend 1 token to give Strong
  Defense instead. Spend 1 more token to protect yourself instead (so self-protection with Strong costs 2).

Trapper
  Night: may place a trap on one player other than yourself and other than the player your trap is already on, or
  may remove your trap (removing it isn't a visit). You have one trap at a time; a new trap replaces the old one at
  once. Traps last even after you die. A trap is armed from the next night on: the first time a
  non-Village player visits the trapped player on a later night, the trap is used up and that visitor is dealt a
  Strong Attack. If several non-Village players visit that night, one of them, chosen at random, is hit. You learn
  that your trap went off, and nothing else (not who, nor whether it killed).

Vigilante
  Tokens: 3 vigilante tokens.
  Night: may shoot one player: 1 token for a Basic Attack, 2 for Standard, 3 for Strong.

Veteran
  Tokens: 3 alert tokens.
  You can't be role-blocked.
  Night: may spend a token to go on alert (not a visit, R6). While on alert you have Standard Defense and you deal
  a Strong Attack to every player who visits you that night, whatever their faction
  (a role-blocker who visits you is attacked too), however many there are. Astral Visits aren't visits, so they
  aren't attacked. At the end of the night you learn whether anyone visited you and, if so, how many, and whether
  your attack on each of them succeeded, but nothing about who they were.

Misc

Prosecutor
  Tokens: 2 prosecutor tokens.
  Night: may spend a token to prosecute someone. The next Day, when nominations would start, the moderator
  announces that someone is being prosecuted; this counts as a successful nomination and they go to trial. It
  counts toward the Day's three-nomination limit, an execution ends the Day as usual, and it can combine with a
  Clerk's rig. If the trial needs a "nominator", the moderator picks a random player.

Party Animal
  Immune to role-blocks.
  Night: may visit one player, role-blocking them.

Philanthropist
  Tokens: 2 philanthropy tokens.
  Night: may spend a token to hand another player one token of their own role's kind (e.g. a medic token to a
  Medic; one kind chosen at random if their role has several). They are told a Philanthropist handed them a token.
  If their role has no tokens, they are told they threw it away because they have no use for it, so it doesn't
  count toward their tokens (e.g. for the Accountant). You are not told which happened.

Outsiders (Village, but the role can hurt the Village)

Roleplayer
  You are told your role is X, a random Village Investigative role other than Eavesdropper or Archivist, chosen at
  the start.
  You aren't told that you're a Roleplayer. The moderator gives you results that are usually false but plausible.
  Exact-role checks see you as Roleplayer (R19). Your night actions only happen in your imagination: they aren't
  visits, have no effect, and no other role sees them.

Ex-Con
  Appears: Evil to alignment checks (R19).
  Night: every night you visit a random player other than yourself, and you aren't told whom. A role-block stops
  the visit (R7).

Cardinal
  If you die, a random living Good player (if any) is dealt a Divine Attack at the end of the night: the night you
  died, or the night after the Day you died. It comes after all of that night's other actions and attacks, so the
  victim is drawn from the Good players still alive then, and it counts as a death that night.

---------------------------------------- Yakuza (Evil) ----------------------------------------
All Yakuza share the Katana and night chat (R8).

Grunt
  Night: none of its own.

Pharmacist
  Immune to role-blocks.
  Night: may role-block one player.

Ritualist
  Tokens: 2 ritual tokens.
  Night: may guess one player's exact role (Astral Visit), spending 1 token for a Standard Attack or 2 for a
  Divine Attack. If right, the Attack is dealt. If wrong, the moderator publicly announces at the start of the
  next Day that you are a Ritualist.

Puppeteer
  Tokens: 1 puppet token.
  Night: may spend a token to puppet someone (not yourself or another Yakuza) for the following Day. That Day, they
  are told they are being puppeted, and they can't chat or see private messages; nobody else is told. You chat
  publicly and privately in their place (including seeing their private messages); that is all you can do for
  them. They still nominate, vote and so on as normal. It lasts one Day.

Fabricator
  Tokens: 1 fabricator token.
  Night: may visit one non-Yakuza player to frame them (no token needed): that night they appear Evil to
  alignment checks (R19); other results are unchanged. On the same visit, you may spend a token to forge that
  player's last will (not anyone else's): if they die of any cause that night, your forged will replaces theirs.
  Framing and forging together are one visit.

Clerk
  Tokens: 1 clerk token.
  Night: may spend a token to rig the next Day's votes. On a rigged Day, anyone successfully nominated is executed.
  The Guilty/Innocent vote still happens; if a majority votes Guilty, the rig is not announced, otherwise it is
  announced publicly.

Smuggler
  Unique: at most one per game. Immune to role-blocks.
  Tokens: 2 swap tokens.
  Night: may spend a token to swap two different living players (yourself included) for the night (R23). You visit
  both of them. Both swapped players are told they were swapped, but not with whom.

Ballot Stuffer
  Tokens: 2 ballot tokens.
  Night: may spend a token so that during the following Day your vote counts twice in every Guilty/Innocent trial
  vote. It does not count twice in nomination (Yes/No) votes, which stay one vote each. A trial that Day needs a
  majority of all votes counted, so your extra vote counts whether you vote Guilty or Innocent. Every player's vote is still revealed as usual; the announced totals
  include your extra vote without saying whose it is.

Diviner
  Night: may pick one player. If they are Yakuza, they get Strong Defense for that night. Otherwise, the group is
  that player plus their nearest neighbor on each side in the circle (R11) among the players alive at the start of
  the night, skipping yourself and never counting anyone twice. You are told who was in the group and the exact
  role of one of them (exact-role check, R19), but not whose it is: it is the player you picked with 60% chance,
  otherwise one of the others (20% each, or 40% if there is only one other). Yakuza in the group and players who
  die that night can be drawn too.

Mortician
  Tokens: 2 mortician tokens.
  Night: may spend a token to try to clean someone. If they die that night by any cause, they are cleaned: their
  death is revealed publicly with its cause, but their role and last will show as "cleaned by mortician". You are
  privately told their role and last will. The token is spent even if they survive.

Wiretap
  You passively read the contents of every private message sent during the Day, between any two players. This is
  not a night action or a visit, so role-blocks don't stop it. Puppeted messages reach you as the puppet's (you
  aren't told about the puppeting). Nobody is told that a Wiretap exists.

---------------------------------------- Neutral ----------------------------------------
Each Neutral player is their own faction (R2).

Serial Killer
  Defense: Standard. Appears: Not Evil to the Sheriff.
  Win: only alone (not even with another Serial Killer).
  Night: may deal a Strong Attack to one player.

Arsonist
  Defense: Standard. Appears: Not Evil to the Sheriff.
  Win: only alone (not even with another Arsonist).
  Night: may douse one player (they aren't told), or ignite (Astral Visit), dealing a Strong Attack to every
  doused player.

Jester
  Defense: Basic. Appears: Evil to the Sheriff.
  Win: iff you are hanged during a Day (other daytime deaths don't count). You lose if the game ends with you
  alive.
  Revenge: if you are hanged, the following night a random Guilty voter from your trial who was alive when that
  night began is dealt a Divine Attack (Astral Visit). If nobody voted Guilty, nothing happens.

Executioner
  Defense: Basic. Appears: Evil to the Sheriff.
  At the start you get a random target: any player except yourself.
  Win: iff your target is hanged during a Day while you are alive (other daytime deaths don't count). You lose if
  the game ends with your target alive.
  On winning: the moderator reveals you at once and you leave the game. Leaving works exactly like dying (you
  leave the circle for good) except it doesn't count as a death. The following night, a random Guilty voter from
  the trial who was alive when that night began (not you, since you left) is dealt a Divine Attack (Astral Visit). If nobody voted
  Guilty, nothing happens.
  If your target dies any other way, you become a Jester.

Vulture
  Unique: at most one Vulture per game.
  Win: alone, if you are alive at the start of a Day (after the night's deaths) and max(floor(N/4), 2) or fewer
  players are alive, counting yourself, where N is the number of players at the start of the game. The game then
  ends at once and everyone else loses (Jester and Executioner wins already earned still count, R1).
  Night: none.
