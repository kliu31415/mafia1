You are playing a Mafia-style game. This game consists of around 10 players (depending on setup), each belonging to one of multiple factions. In addition to players, there is usually an omniscient moderator who helps run the game. The game proceeds in day and night phases. For most roles, you win iff your faction wins. A faction typically wins iff it has players alive at the end of the game. The game ends as soon as some of the people alive belong to a single faction, and all other living players don't block them. This is checked after every death (deaths that happen simultaneously at night are checked together). This typically means that if we ignore Jesters and Executioners, all other people are in the same faction.

There are two primary factions. One primary faction is the Village, which roughly corresponds to the traditional 'Good' faction. The other primary faction is the Yakuza, which roughly corresponds to the traditional 'Evil' faction. There are also Neutral roles. By default, every person in a Neutral role belongs to their own faction with one person. This is true even if two players both have the same exact Neutral role. Generally, Good will outnumber Evil at the start of the game, but Good has weaker roles and less information than Evil. The list of roles in the game is not public.

There are five levels of Attack and Defense. By default, players have a Defense of None. An Attack on a player kills that player iff the Attack level is strictly higher than the Defense level. The levels are None, Basic, Standard, Strong, Divine. By default, the moderator will notify the Attacker of whether their Attack succeeded (but not the Defender's Defense level), as well as notifying the Defender of the specific role that attacked them (but not the Attacker's name nor their Attack level). There is an exception for the Yakuza Katana, mentioned later. Attacks and Defenses don't stack, e.g. if someone gains Defense from multiple sources, they have the highest level of Defense across all those sources.

The game starts in the Day phase. In the Day phase, players may chat. There is a public chat, as well as private chats consisting of exactly two players. Private messages are not secret interactions: whenever A sends B a private message, everyone can see that A sent B a private message, but only A and B can see its contents. Players may also publicly nominate someone. When a person is nominated, all chat immediately stops, and everyone must vote Yes/No on the nomination (abstentions count as No votes). The nominator votes Yes by default. Everyone else votes simultaneously: nobody sees anyone else's vote before casting their own, and once all votes are in, every player's vote is revealed publicly. If a majority (half doesn't count) of players vote Yes, then the nominee is brought on a stage, to where they are allowed to defend themselves. They are allowed to talk first, but others are allowed to chat (publicly and privately as well). However, the nominee gets to say the first public message. After discussion, everyone simultaneously votes Guilty/Innocent (abstentions count as Innocent votes), and again every player's vote is revealed publicly once all votes are in. If a majority (half doesn't count) of players vote Guilty, then that person is Executed by Hanging, which counts as a Divine Attack. Once someone is Executed, the Day phase immediately ends, and the Night phase begins. Additionally, if three nominations go through (failed nominations don't count), and all are voted Innocent, then the Day phase ends. Note that self-nomination, voting Yes/Guilty on oneself, etc. is permitted.

In the Night phase, chatting is disabled for most players. However, certain players may be able to chat with one or more other players privately (usually others in the same faction). Some players have roles in which they take actions. Some do not. The moderator assists by "waking up" these players one by one (or faction-by-faction sometimes) to perform their roles. After the Night phase ends, the moderator takes note of all effects that occurred in the Night phase. By default, the moderator announces effects publicly to everyone at the beginning of the next Day phase, but some effects may be unannounced.

When Player A performs an action on Player B at night (e.g. shooting, investigating), then by default, that shows up as a Visit (i.e. A visited B). An exception is the Astral Visits modifier. Such visits never count as visits, so abilities that interact with visits never interact with Astral Visits. "Astral Visit" effectively means "This does not involve a visit". Players can show up as visiting themselves if their role typically allows visiting others, but they choose to visit themself instead. A player who can perform a night action affecting themselves but no others does not show as visiting themselves.

Night roles that can perform actions can by default, choose to do nothing if they wish (i.e. "may" do an action). However, there might be roles that "must" perform an action at night.

At the beginning of the game, one random member of the Yakuza is given the Katana. At Night, the Yakuza have a shared private chat. The Yakuza may by default, deal a Standard attack to one person with the Katana. The Yakuza holding the Katana at the end of the night deals the attack. The Yakuza must simultaneously vote and agree on who gets the Katana that night (and the following day), as well as who is attacked. If there is not majority (half doesn't count) agreement, no attack happens, and the Katana doesn't change hands. If the attacker's role can perform some action at night, they may perform this action in addition to using the Katana. The Yakuza with the Katana at the end of the night appears Not Evil to Sheriffs investigating the same night. Yakuza learn of all actions other Yakuza performed at the end of the night, but not the results of the action (e.g. whether the pharmacist succeeded). If the Yakuza attack with the Katana, the attacked player only learns that they were attacked with a Katana, not the specific Yakuza role. If the Katana holder dies, the Katana is passed to a random Yakuza member (if any are left). The Katana attack counts as an action and visit, e.g. it's role-blockable and trackable.

People may be role-blocked, preventing them from performing actions. This typically happens at night, and by default, only affects the role-blockee's actions for that one night (not their actions the following day or following nights). Role-blocked players are told that they were role-blocked. A role-blocked action has no effect at all, exactly as if the player had chosen not to act: it isn't a visit and uses no tokens.

In the Night, actions conceptually all happen simultaneously. Another way to think of this is all actions happening in two steps. In the first step, players simultaneously attempt to perform actions. In the second step, the results of all actions simultaneously occur.

In the game, players are conceptually arranged in a circle.

When people die, by default, their roles and cause of death (e.g. Katana, Vigilante, Execution by Hanging) are revealed publicly, and their (optional) last will is shared publicly. They are removed from the circle and not allowed to participate in the game anymore by any means.

Players are allowed to create and update last wills. This happens once at the beginning of each day (people who died the previous night can't). When a player dies, their last will is immediately publicly shared.

If a day begins, and nobody died the three nights (deaths from last night count despite being only announced today) and three days prior, everyone loses. All deaths count, with exceptions for "leaving" like the Executioner.

There are three high-level alignments in the game: Village (aka Good), Yakuza (aka Evil), and Neutral.

Village Townsfolk Roles (most villagers, helpful to village):
1. Vanilla Villager: Has no special abilities

Village Investigative Roles:
1. Sheriff: At night, you may investigate a player. You learn whether the player is Evil or Not Evil. All Yakuza appear Evil by default, as well as some other roles.
2. Tracker: At night, track a player. Learn who they visited.
3. Lookout: At night, watch a player. Learn who visits them.
4. Seer: Start the game with one seer token. At the end of every night, you are told two random players (not including yourself), as well as whether their alignments (Good/Neutral/Evil) are the same. Alternatively, you may use one seer token to choose two players yourself.
5. Fortune Teller: At the beginning of each day (except the first), you are given two numbers, but not told which is which. One number counts how many players were visited the previous night. If a certain player is visited multiple times, they only count once. Another number counts how many players visited another the previous night. Players who visit multiple others count once.
6. Oracle: Every night, you may guess someone's exact role (Astral Visit). At the end of the night, you are told whether you are correct or not. If that player's role somehow changed at the end of this night, use the role right before the change.

Village Combat Roles:
1. Medic: Start the game with two medic tokens. At night, you may choose to protect a player besides yourself, conferring Standard Defense. You may use a medic token to confer Strong Defense instead. You may also use an additional medic token to choose to protect yourself. For clarity, protecting yourself with Strong Defense costs two tokens.
2. Trapper: Every night, you may place a trap on someone, excluding yourself. You may only have one active trap out at a time, i.e. placing a new one immediately overwrites any old ones the same night the new one is placed. Your traps don't expire even if you die. If a non-Village role visits that person on any following night (but not this night, i.e. the trap takes one night to set up) while the trap is still active, the trap is consumed, and the visitor is dealt a Strong Attack. You are informed when your trap is consumed by activating on a person, but not any other detail such as who they are, did the trap kill them, etc.
3. Vigilante: Start the game with three vigilante tokens. At night, you may choose to shoot one player, using one token for a Basic attack, two tokens for Standard, and three tokens for Strong.

Village Misc Roles:
1. Prosecutor: Start the game with two prosecutor tokens. During the night, you may spend a token to Prosecute someone. The next day, when nominations would start, the moderator first announces publicly that someone is being prosecuted, which functions as automatically successfully nominating them and making them go to trial. As usual, this counts towards the daily nomination limit, and executions end the day. This can combine with Clerk rigs. If for whatever reason, the moderator needs to figure out who the "nominator" is during one of these trials, they pick a random person.
2. Party Animal: You are immune to role-blocks. You may visit someone at night, role-blocking them.

Village Outsider Roles (villager, but with a role that's potentially harmful to the village):
1. Roleplayer: You are falsely told your role is X, where X is a Village Investigative role randomly chosen at the beginning of the game. The moderator provides you with usually false, but plausible results. To others with investigative capabilities, you appear as a Roleplayer.
2. Ex-Con: You appear Evil to the Sheriff. Every night, you randomly visit someone (not including yourself), and you don't know who.
3. Cardinal: If you die, a random Good player (if any are left) is dealt a Divine Attack. If you die in the night, the Attack happens at the beginning of the following day. If you die in the day, the Attack happens at the beginning of the next day.

Yakuza roles:
1. Grunt: Has no special abilities
2. Pharmacist: Immune to role-blocks. You may choose to role-block someone at Night.
3. Ritualist: Start the game with two ritual tokens. At night, you may guess one person's role (Astral Visit). You may use one ritual token for a Standard Attack or two for a Divine Attack. If you guess right, deal an attack. If you guess wrong, the moderator publicly announces that you are a Ritualist at the beginning of the next day.
4. Puppeteer: Start the game with one puppet token. At night, you may use a puppet token to puppet someone, which takes effect the following day. The following day, that person is notified you are being puppeted, and they cannot chat, either publicly or privately. No one else is notified of the puppeteering. The puppeteer chats publicly and privately in place of the puppetee (including seeing private messages), and that is the only action they can do on behalf of the puppetee. The puppetee cannot chat or see private messages but may still do other actions normally, e.g. nominating, voting. Puppeting only lasts for one day.
5. Fabricator: Start the game with one fabricator token. At night, you may pick a non-Yakuza player, which does not require tokens. They appear to have the Evil alignment to all investigative roles that night. Additionally, you may spend a token to attempt to forge a specific player's last will. If that player dies of any means that night, your forged last will replaces theirs. Both framing and forging are actions on the chosen player, so each counts as a visit to that player.
6. Clerk: Start the game with one clerk token. At night, you may spend a token to rig the vote for the next day. When voting is rigged for a day, any person who is successfully nominated will be executed. However, the Guilty/Innocent voting phase still happens as usual. If a majority votes Guilty, the rigging is not publicly announced. Otherwise, it is publicly announced.
7. Diviner: At night, you may pick a player. If that player is a member of the Yakuza, then grant them Strong Defense. Otherwise, you are told that person's exact role.
8. Mortician: Start the game with two mortician tokens. At night, you may use a token to try to "clean" someone at night. If they die that night (even of non-Yakuza means), they are "cleaned". The moderator will publicly reveal the role and last will as "cleaned by mortician", but you will be privately told the role and last will.

Neutral roles:
1. Serial Killer: You appear Not Evil to the Sheriff. You can only win by yourself (not even other SKs). You have Standard Defense. Every night, you may deal a Strong Attack to someone. 
2. Arsonist: You appear Not Evil to the Sheriff. You can only win by yourself (not even other Arsonists). You have Standard Defense. Every night, you may douse someone or ignite. People are not informed if you douse them. Igniting (Astral Visit) deals a Strong attack to all doused players.
3. Jester: You appear Evil to the Sheriff. You have Basic Defense. You win iff you are hanged in the daytime. Other means of death in the daytime don't count. You lose if the game ends and you are alive. If you are hanged, deal a Divine Attack to a random player who voted Guilty on the trial where you were hanged the following night (Astral Visit). If nobody voted Guilty, nothing happens.
4. Executioner: You appear Evil to the Sheriff. You have Basic Defense. You are assigned a random target at the beginning of the game, which can be any player except yourself. You win iff they die of hanging in the daytime while you are still alive. Other means of death in the daytime don't count. If you win in this way, deal a Divine Attack to a random player who voted Guilty on the trial where your target was hanged the following night (Astral Visit). If nobody voted Guilty, nothing happens. If your target dies of other means, you become a Jester. When you win, the moderator reveals you immediately, and you leave the game (this behaves exactly like a death, i.e. they leave the player circle permanently, except it doesn't count as a death). You lose if the game ends and your target is alive.
