That made them the only standout players, so on Night 1 three investigators targeted them: Carol tracked Bob, Erin checked Bob as Sheriff, and Dave watched Grace as Lookout. The Yakuza then skipped their Night 1 kill, which gave the Village a free round of checks. On Day 2, Bob was asked about eight times whether he had held the Katana (the Yakuza weapon, whose holder reads Not Evil to a Sheriff). He never gave a yes or no, and that refusal became the whole case against him. Frank, the Jester, nominated himself with no data. The town saw it as bait (Alice named it a likely Jester play), and the nomination got only Frank's own Yes vote. On Night 2 the Yakuza made two good moves: they killed Carol, the claimed Tracker, and Grace's Pharmacist block hit Erin, who was checking Grace that night. But Grace's Sleepwalker trait sent her on a random visit to Alice, a Veteran on alert, and the counterattack killed her. That left Bob alone. Dave's Lookout result (Carol and Grace visited Erin) matched Erin being role-blocked and Carol's Tracker claim. Erin then nominated Bob for his evasions. Bob's defense rested on the false idea that Grace could not have visited both Erin and Alice, which a Sleepwalker visit easily explains. He was hanged 5-1. Village mistakes: Carol publicly claimed Tracker on Day 2 and was killed that night. No one spelled out the strongest mechanical read: Erin was blocked and only Carol and Grace visited her, so Grace was the blocker and likely Yakuza.

- **deception, for Yakuza:** Plan an answer to the obvious yes/no questions (such as 'Did you hold the Katana?') before Day 2. Then answer briefly and consistently. Dodging the same direct question for a whole Day becomes the case against you, even if no result says you are Evil. _(Bob was asked about eight times on Day 2 whether he held the Katana and replied each time with 'no claim, I want data.' On Day 3, Erin nominated him because of that refusal, and Alice, Dave, Erin and Heidi all named it in their Guilty reasoning.)_
- **coordination, for Yakuza:** Never send daytime private messages to a teammate. Everyone can see that a message was sent, and on early days it is often the only event, so it decides who the investigators check that night. Keep all plans for night chat. _(Bob sent Grace a private message on Day 1, and Carol pointed it out publicly. That night Carol tracked Bob, Erin checked Bob and Dave watched Grace. On Day 3, Heidi and Alice asked Bob to explain the private messages.)_
- **night actions, for Yakuza:** Don't skip the Night 1 kill in a small game. Each night without a kill gives every investigator a free result and takes nothing from the Village. Kill a vocal player or the person driving suspicion toward your team. _(The Yakuza agreed on 'no kill, no block' on Night 1. Carol, who had pressed Bob and Grace on Day 1, survived to claim Tracker. All three investigators got a free look at the Yakuza pair.)_
- **deduction, for Village:** Combine a block report with the Lookout's list of visitors. If a player says they were role-blocked and the Lookout saw only one visitor whose action is unexplained, that visitor is very likely the blocker. A blocker is most often the Yakuza Pharmacist, so their daytime private-message partner becomes the top suspect. _(On Night 2, Erin was role-blocked and Dave saw only Carol (claimed Tracker) and Grace visit her. So Grace was the blocker, which tied Bob to the Yakuza through his private messages to her. No one stated this chain. Bob was hanged on behavior alone.)_
- **rules and mechanics, for everyone:** A player can visit more than one person in a night: their own action plus a Sleepwalker visit or a Katana attack. Before calling a claim impossible because 'X only visited Y,' check whether a second visit could explain it. _(In his trial, Bob argued that Alice's Veteran kill of Grace contradicted Dave's report that Grace visited Erin. Dave, Erin and Heidi answered that a second visit, for example from a Sleepwalker, explains it, and this was in fact what happened. His defense collapsed and the vote was 5-1.)_
- **night actions, for Veteran:** Use alerts on the early nights, before you claim, when visits are spread around randomly. After an alert kill, claim Veteran the next Day so the town reads the dead visitor correctly. _(Alice alerted on Nights 1 and 2 without claiming. Grace's Sleepwalker visit ran into the Night 2 alert, killing one of the two Yakuza. Alice claimed right away on Day 3, which explained the death and kept the town's attention on Bob.)_
- **persuasion and voting, for Jester:** A self-nomination with no data is the most recognizable Jester move, and it almost never gets a majority. Instead, create mild suspicion through small factual errors or odd votes that make you look like a Yakuza, and push for a nomination only when the town is short of leads. _(Frank nominated himself on Day 2 and got only his own Yes vote. Alice publicly called it 'bait, possibly a Jester play,' and the town stopped considering him.)_
- **deception, for Village investigators:** Hold back a power-role claim until your result can actually decide a vote. A Day 2 claim of a result that decides nothing mostly makes you the night's kill target. _(On Day 2, Carol claimed Tracker with an inconclusive result about Bob, and the Yakuza killed her that night because she was 'the bigger threat.' ## Outside review (claude-opus-5.5)

- **low, harness bug:** Night 2: Grace (Pharmacist) received a private moderator message 'You visited Erin tonight.' R9 says a role-blocker learns only whether the block failed because the target can't be role-blocked. This extra confirmation isn't in the rules. It leaked nothing hidden because she chose the target herself, but it is output the rules don't provide for and could read as a result.
- **low, prompt problem:** Frank's in-game feedback said the action schema requires many fields even when unused, and that the nominate/target fields are typed as nullable strings, which made formatting awkward until he used plain values. This interface friction could cause malformed or mistaken actions.
- **low, player confusion:** Day 2: Frank claimed 'Erin and Carol cannot both be right about a single Bob check without someone lying.' A Sheriff's Not Evil and a Tracker's no-visit result are independent and fully compatible (and both were true). Three players corrected him. As the Jester he may have said it deliberately as bait, but the statement misreads the mechanics.
