# Tips from past games

Written 2026-10-18 08:25 UTC by opus-5.5 with `uv run tips.py build`, from the 424 active entries of tips_notebook.json (103 games). Players never see this preamble; they see every section below. Earlier sheets are in history/. Entries last checked against rules a4bd03b31963e907.

## Most often decisive
- Only the Mayor, Vulture, Hitman and Saint are Unique. A second claimant of any other role is often real, and two real holders tend to pick the same targets. That isn't evidence for either claimant, though. Test both that Day or night (a watch, an exact-role guess, a mark). Don't hang the later one on timing, and don't let both through untested. (seen in 53 games)
- Before voting Guilty, name one corroborated mechanical fit: a claim of a Yakuza-only role, a first-person slip about an action the claim can't make, a knowledge leak, or a claim broken by results others confirm. One unverified result is only a claim, and an impossible one may be Roleplayer noise. When one more night kill could lock the vote, hang the best case you have. If you're Remorseful, a Guilty vote on a hanged Villager kills you. Claim the trait before the vote and vote Guilty only on a case like this. A 'remorse' death proves the hanged player was Village. (seen in 83 games)
- Investigators usually do best posting the target and exact result in their first speech once it bears on a vote. A result held back until after a rival's claim looks retrofitted. While a Ritualist may be alive, write the log in your will first and claim only as much of your role as the result needs. (seen in 48 games)
- Yakuza often lose through daytime PMs. Everyone sees who messaged whom, a PM triangle becomes the town's lead, and PMs while a teammate is under pressure read as vote-buying. Plan in night chat only. (seen in 77 games)
- Yakuza: defending a doomed teammate rarely pays. Totals get compared with stated votes, so a hidden Innocent shows up as a gap. Unless your team's votes can really stop the hang, vote Yes and Guilty and say so. (seen in 68 games)

## Everyone
- Keep a notepad of every player's stated vote on each nomination and trial, the announced totals and your own vote. A mismatch most often comes from a miscast, a lie (often from a Neutral or a Turncoat) or a Merciful or Zealot trait, not a manipulation role. Ask voters once what they cast and how it counted, and never hang on a mismatch alone. Yes counts can't be altered, so a Yes mismatch means someone lied. Once a Fixer is shown, one extra Guilty on a Village claimant's trial suggests the Yakuza wanted that hang. (seen in 25 games)
- Spend one-shot tokens the first time they can swing a vote, because the dead can't use them. Kill tokens are the exception: fire on a break in the target's own claim, not on a claim clash or at a Neutral that never blocks wins. Basic and Standard attacks fail on a Serial Killer's or Arsonist's Standard Defense. At parity, fire even on a coin flip. (seen in 36 games)
- An unexplained Katana survival has many possible causes: a protector, innate Defense, the hidden Narrow Escape trait (it works once), a hidden Shaky holder whose Katana never kills, or a Paranoid trait's made-up notice. A Villager's Stolen Blade kill also reads 'Katana'. Ask once for the exact notice. A survivor who confirms it under a claim with no Defense, with nobody claiming the save, is the Neutral-killer lead. (seen in 28 games)
- A Turncoat (at most one per game) is a Villager who wins with the Yakuza. They read Evil to a Sheriff or Seer, count as Good in a census and show a real Village role to exact-role checks. A Village claimant who keeps protecting or voting to save the main suspect is the likely one. (seen in 2 games)
- A corroborated report that X was the only unexplained visitor to a Katana victim is near-proof that X attacked. A lone, uncorroborated watch is just that claimant's word, above all one that was held back or names the claimant's own accuser. (seen in 52 games)
- Before each night, write your plan and what each possible result would mean in your will. Repeat key results in public too, because a Fabricator can forge the will of a player who dies on a night it visits them. (seen in 56 games)
- Answer a direct question about your night action in one plain sentence the first time it's asked. Repeated evasion becomes a case of its own. (seen in 29 games)

## Village
- Read every cause of death. A Serial Killer, Werewolf or ignite cause proves that killer is alive, and the game can't end while it lives. Keep a written suspect list (unwatched no-action claimants, survivors no protector claims, unexplained visitors) and hang from it while the table is large. Count to the vote lock in effective votes: the Yakuza plus every fixed Innocent (the accused, a declared Merciful voter, a likely Turncoat). Near the lock, hang your best Yakuza lead, because a hanged Neutral doesn't lower the Yakuza count. The Day before the lock is your last real hang, so turn down 'one more night'. (seen in 23 games)
- Once a mechanical result shows someone is Yakuza, look first at the players who defended them, pushed to delay their hang or PMed them. Ask PM pairs in public what was said. A PM link is a lead, not a hang, until it is paired with a mechanical break. A lone Evil Sheriff read or a caught liar isn't that kind of result: Chaos Neutrals, Notorious or Turncoat Villagers and framed players all read Evil. (seen in 57 games)
- Jester signs: self-nominating, voting Yes on your own nomination, pushing hangs with no data, asking for the rope. Prefer to leave such a player alive. If you hang one anyway, agree on a named Guilty list in public and have confirmed power roles vote Innocent, since the revenge kills a random Guilty voter. (seen in 30 games)
- Don't hint at a power role on Day 1 or 2. Never announce tonight's target, or even that you'll act tonight, in public or by PM. Half-hints draw the Katana and gain nothing. (seen in 53 games)

## Yakuza
- Agree a Village cover for each member in Night 1 chat. Expect the real holder to counterclaim and visit results to test you. Pick a cover whose night record you can account for, name your real visits as its targets, and don't switch claims once it breaks. Never claim Vanilla or any Yakuza-only role, not even in a slip. (seen in 35 games)
- Every member should submit the agreed holder and target every night. Open each night-chat message with 'Holder: X. Target: Y. My action: Z', because messages over 200 words get cut. (seen in 45 games)
- Pick kills by what each player could still find out. Census Takers and passive count or group roles usually rank above investigators whose results are already public. A claimed shooter is the protectors' obvious ward, so remove the protector first. On Night 1, a quiet, capable player is usually a better target than the loud organizer, whom protection stacks on. Give the Katana to the member you can best afford to lose. (seen in 43 games)
- After a failed Katana attack, assume protection or Defense and remove the protector rather than retrying blindly. If the same holder's attacks fail on different targets, that member may be Shaky, so move the Katana. (seen in 42 games)
- Use one-shot tokens (cleans, forges, fixes, doubled ballots, ritual guesses) early. Holders who wait for a 'decisive Day' tend to die with them unused. (seen in 30 games)

## Sheriff
- The Katana holder reads Not Evil on the night they hold it, so a Not Evil read on a suspected attacker clears little. Once only one Yakuza can be left, it clears nobody. (seen in 29 games)

## Coroner
- Examine a body whose role settles a live question (one side of a duplicate claim, a hanged suspect) on the first night one exists, and post the night, target and role at once. An undisputed Katana victim is rarely worth a token, and a cleaned body tells you nothing. (seen in 4 games)

## Medic
- Guard the likeliest Katana targets, such as claimed investigators, and never visit a possible Veteran. Spend Strong tokens once a Serial Killer or Arsonist cause appears, and protect yourself the night after you claim. (seen in 43 games)

## Vigilante
- Dying with unused tokens is the usual failure, so set a trigger by Day 2-3 and fire a Basic shot on a real lead. Once only a Standard-Defense Neutral killer is left, a Basic or Standard shot can only kill Villagers: fire Strong or hold. (seen in 31 games)
