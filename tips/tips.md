# Tips from past games

Written 2026-10-10 23:30 by opus-5.5 with `uv run tips.py build`, from the 249 active entries of tips_notebook.json (46 games). Players never see this preamble; they see every section below. Earlier sheets are in history/.

## Everyone
- Ballots are secret, so keep a notepad: for every nomination and trial, log each player's stated vote, the announced totals and your own vote. Flag stated votes that later change, contradict the player's reads or don't fit the totals.
- Totals can be changed on purpose. A Ballot Stuffer makes a trial total exceed the voter count, and a Fixer silently flips one player's trial votes. Neither touches nomination votes, so a Yes count that contradicts stated votes is the cleanest proof of a liar; list the fix and stuffer accounts before accusing on trial totals.
- Keep your votes consistent with your stated reads, and explain any change before the vote. A read/vote mismatch in others is a lead, not a verdict. (seen in 30 games)
- A visit shows a destination, not an action. Still, a corroborated lone unexplained visitor to a Katana victim is near-proof against them; don't let 'a visit isn't an attack' acquit them. (seen in 29 games)
- Get the mechanics right before calling a contradiction: actions are optional and roles can repeat unless Unique. But 'roles can repeat' only means a claim isn't disproved; it is not evidence for it. (seen in 28 games)
- Before each night, write your planned target in your will, and post results publicly by day. The result of the night you die never reaches your will. (seen in 27 games)
- Answer a direct question about your night action in one plain sentence the first time it is asked. Evasion becomes its own case. (seen in 7 games)
- Tokens are useless once you're dead. Spend one-shot tokens on the first Day or night they can change a vote or remove a threat. A puppet or doubled ballot lapses if you die the night you spend it.

## Village
- Investigators: post your role, night, target and exact result in your first speech after getting it. If someone copies your claim, counterclaim at once with your full log. (seen in 16 games)
- Before voting Guilty, name a concrete contradiction or mechanical fit: an Evil result against a Vanilla claim, a group elimination, or a lone visitor. If the nominee self-nominated, wants the rope, or the case is only behavioural, run the Jester check first. (seen in 32 games)
- If the nominee wins by being hanged, leave them alive. If you must hang a possible Jester, agree on a bare Guilty majority and keep confirmed power roles on Innocent. (seen in 15 games)
- Once a player is shown Yakuza by a mechanical result, suspect those who argued for No or delay, or who PM'd them. Ask for stated votes before each vote and compare them with the Yes count. A PM link alone is a lead, never a hang. (seen in 28 games)
- On Day 2 don't hint at protective roles or tokens. Never announce tonight's target, in public or by PM; that tells the Pharmacist and the Katana where to go. (seen in 24 games)
- Hunt Neutral killers on purpose. A cause of death not explained by the Katana or known shooters, a multi-death night, or an unexplained survival means one is alive. Point checks at unwatched Vanilla and unclaimed players, and hang the best suspect before you are down to two players.
- Survival confirms nothing: Arsonists and Serial Killers have Standard Defense, and Jesters and Executioners have Basic. Ask the survivor which protector explains it. (seen in 15 games)
- An archive is a sample, not a census: about half the entries are fake. Use it to decide whom to press and test, then hang on the contradiction the test produces. (seen in 14 games)

## Yakuza
- Never PM teammates by day; plan everything in night chat. An Eavesdropper or Wiretap reads PMs, and PM triangles become the town's lead. (seen in 30 games)
- Don't defend a doomed teammate unless your team's votes can actually save them. Otherwise vote with the town. On a likely Jester, keep some teammates on Innocent to stay out of the revenge draw. (seen in 34 games)
- Every member submits the agreed Katana holder and target every night. With two Yakuza, one missing vote cancels the kill. (seen in 19 games)
- Fake-claim a Village role whose tokens match yours. Never claim a Yakuza-only role, a Unique role or a role already claimed in public. Fix each member's claim before Day 2 and never switch it. (seen in 18 games)
- Give the Katana to your least useful member. Move it to an unexposed member the night a Lookout or Tracker names the holder, and kill info roles whose results are narrowing your options. (seen in 21 games)
- Never flatly deny a visit someone could have seen. Admit it at once with a cover story agreed in night chat. (seen in 15 games)
- After a failed attack, kill or block the likely protector instead of retrying. Use the rig and puppet early, but never rig a Day when a teammate is the likely nominee. (seen in 19 games)
- Smuggler swaps move exactly the actions aimed at the two swapped players. Aim the Katana at a decoy and swap the decoy with the protected real target, or swap yourself with a Villager to dodge a check. Never swap the Katana's target by accident.

## Sheriff
- The Katana holder reads Not Evil that night, and a lone last Yakuza reads Not Evil every night. Say so whenever you post a Not Evil result. (seen in 16 games)

## Coroner
- Examine bodies that settle a live question: one side of a duplicate claim, a hanged suspect, or a possibly forged will. Post night, target and role in one message. Skip cleaned bodies, which show nothing.

## Medic
- Free Standard protection stops the Katana. Protect vocal analysts and claimed investigators, rotate among them, and self-protect with a token right after you claim. (seen in 21 games)

## Vigilante
- Don't die holding your shots. Set a trigger by Day 2-3 and fire a Basic shot on a solid lead, then post the night, target and role if it kills. (seen in 11 games)

## Arsonist
- Ignite once 3 or more living players are doused, or with 2 if anyone is questioning you. Every night spent dousing is a night the town can hang you first. (seen in 12 games)
