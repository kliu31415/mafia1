# Tips from past games

Written 2026-10-18 09:35 UTC by opus-5.5 with `uv run tips.py build`, from the 427 active entries of tips_notebook.json (104 games). Players never see this preamble; they see every section below. Earlier sheets are in history/. Entries last checked against rules 7c0f9435c3bc058c.

## Most often decisive
- Only the Mayor, Magistrate, Vulture, Hitman and Saint are Unique, so a second claimant of another role is often real. Tables keep hanging real duplicate Kitsunes, Census Takers and Enforcers as 'copiers'. But the fact that roles can repeat is no evidence for either claimant. Test both that Day or night (a watch, an exact-role check, a mark). Don't hang on timing, and don't let the pair through untested. (seen in 54 games)
- Before you vote Guilty, name a corroborated mechanical case: a first-person slip, a knowledge leak, a group result whose other members are confirmed, or a corroborated lone visitor. A public claim of a Yakuza-only role is effectively a confession, so hang that player the same Day. One unverified claimant's map, watch or count is a claim, not a case. If the nominee invites the rope, run the Jester check first. Near the vote lock, hang the best case you have. If you are Remorseful, a Guilty vote on a hanged Villager kills you too, so hold to this bar. A 'remorse' death proves the hanged player was Village, so look again at who built that case. (seen in 84 games)
- Yakuza: plan in night chat, not in Day PMs. A hidden Wiretap or Eavesdropper may read PMs, and everyone sees who messaged whom. On a Day a teammate is under pressure, any PM reads as vote-buying. If a PM is already on record, agree one dull summary that you both give. (seen in 78 games)
- Once a mechanical result shows a player is Yakuza, whoever defended them, argued to delay their hang or PM'd them is usually your next suspect. The telling pattern is a No on the nomination, then a Guilty once the hang is certain. A caught liar or a lone Evil read is not 'shown Yakuza': Jesters lie, and they read Evil. (seen in 58 games)
- Investigators: once a result bears on a vote, post the target and the exact result in your first speech. A result held back until someone counterclaims tends to look retrofitted. Naming your role feeds a possible Ritualist, so put your full log in your will first, and keep the role quiet while you have no result. (seen in 48 games)

## Everyone
- Ballots are secret, so keep a notepad: every Yes count, every trial total and each player's stated vote. Vote the way you said you would (re-read your reminder), and explain any switch before the vote. A stated vote that doesn't fit a small total is usually a miscast: ask the player once, and don't hang on it. Before you name a Fixer, rule out a lie (often from a Neutral or a Turncoat) and a Merciful or Zealot trait. Nothing can alter nomination Yes counts, so they are the cleanest check. If a Fixer has actually been shown, an extra Guilty on a Village claimant's trial points at whoever pushed that hang. (seen in 38 games)
- Spend one-shot tokens the first time they can swing a vote or remove a threat: tokens die with you, and Magpies steal them. Kill tokens need a lead first: a break in the target's own claim, votes or visits. A claim clash or a 'test' of a Medic doesn't count. Check your attack level against Standard Defense, and at parity fire even on a coin flip. (seen in 37 games)
- Survivals and Katana notices often prove less than they seem. A hidden Shaky holder's Katana never kills, Narrow Escape saves any player once, a Villager's Stolen Blade kill reads 'Katana', and the Paranoid trait fakes attack notices. Ask who protected the survivor, compare failed attacks across targets, and keep Serial Killers and Arsonists in the pool. (seen in 28 games)
- A visit shows a destination, not an action. A corroborated lone visitor to a Katana victim is near-proof. An uncorroborated watch or map is only its claimant's word, above all if it was held back or names the claimant's own accuser. (seen in 52 games)
- Answer a direct question about your night action in one plain sentence the first time it's asked. Before each night, write your plan and what each result would mean into your will. A result from the night you die never reaches your will, and wills can be forged, so post key results in public too. (seen in 56 games)

## Village
- Read every cause of death: a Serial Killer, Werewolf or ignite cause proves a win-blocking killer is alive. Keep a written suspect list (unclaimed players, unwatched players, unexplained survivors) and aim your checks at it. Hanging that killer doesn't lower the Yakuza count, so near the lock hang your Yakuza lead. While the Katana still kills, don't spend hangs on Neutrals that never block wins. (seen in 24 games)
- On Days 1-2, don't hint at a power role. Never announce tonight's target or that you will act, in public or by PM: it tells the blocker and the Katana where to go. (seen in 53 games)
- Jester signs: pushing with no data, self-nominating, inviting the rope, and reckless lies that are easy to check. An Evil read on such a player fits a Jester. If you hang one anyway, agree in public on a bare named Guilty majority and have power roles vote Innocent. A 'Jester' who begs for Innocent is usually fake, and a hang with no Jester win announcement proves it. (seen in 31 games)
- A Not Evil read clears nothing for the player who held the Katana that night, and once only one Yakuza is left it clears no one. Earlier in the game, rank never-checked players as more suspicious than Not Evil readers. (seen in 29 games)
- Count down to the vote lock in effective votes: the Yakuza plus every fixed Innocent (the nominee, a Merciful voter, a likely Turncoat). The Day before the lock is your last real hang, so put up your strongest Yakuza case and refuse 'one more night'. A Turncoat holds a real Village role, reads Evil to a Sheriff or Seer but counts Good in a census, and wins only with the Yakuza. Count a player who keeps protecting the main suspect and voting to save them as a Yakuza vote. If you are the Turncoat, work out the Yakuza from public results and offer your help in one short PM. (seen in 19 games)

## Yakuza
- Don't defend a doomed teammate unless the team's votes can actually save them. Vote Yes and Guilty with the town, and state the vote you really cast: hidden Innocents show up in the totals. (seen in 69 games)
- Start each night-chat message with 'Holder: X. Target: Y. My action: Z' (messages are cut at 200 words), and make sure every member votes. With two Yakuza, one missing vote cancels the kill. (seen in 45 games)
- Agree covers on Night 1. Each should be a real Village role that fits that member's tokens and visits: never Vanilla, Grunt or a Yakuza-only role. Check each cover against every posted visit result. A broken cover followed by a switch usually gets that member hanged. (seen in 35 games)
- Pick kills by what the player will produce next: Census Takers first, then counting, group and exact-role checkers, ahead of investigators whose results are already public. On Night 1 protectors stack on the loudest voice, so a quiet, capable target and an expendable holder tend to work better. (seen in 44 games)

## Coroner
- Examine the body that settles a live question (one side of a duplicate claim, a hanged suspect) on the first night it exists. Post the night, target and role in one message, and don't announce tomorrow's exam. (seen in 4 games)

## Medic
- Guard the likely Katana targets, but never a possible Veteran. Your free Standard guard doesn't stop Strong attacks (a Serial Killer, an ignite). Don't announce your ward, spend a token to protect yourself after a public claim, and don't die holding tokens. (seen in 43 games)

## Vigilante
- Fire a Basic shot on a lead by Day 2-3, while the Village still has numbers. Dying with all your tokens is the usual failure. If only a Neutral killer is left late in the game, Basic and Standard shots can't kill it and an Avenger's hit-back can kill you, so fire Strong or leave it to the hang. (seen in 31 games)
