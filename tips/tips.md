# Tips from past games

Written 2026-10-17 12:57 UTC by opus-5.5 with `uv run tips.py build`, from the 412 active entries of tips_notebook.json (100 games). Players never see this preamble; they see every section below. Earlier sheets are in history/. Entries last checked against rules 8f2138dae76eb0b8.

## Most often decisive
- Only the Mayor, Vulture, Hitman and Saint are Unique, so two claimants of any other role can both be real. Real duplicates tend to pick the same targets and get matching results, and towns have hanged the real one for 'copying'. Both can also be fake. Don't hang on a clash or on claim timing. Test both that Day or night (a target with known visits, an exact-role check, a watch) and look for a detail the later claimant couldn't have copied. (seen in 51 games)
- Before voting Guilty, name a concrete break whose result is itself corroborated by the players it names, a second source or the claimant's proven record. One unverified map, watch or count is only a claim. An impossible result is often Roleplayer noise: throw out the result, not the player. If your own results clash with a confirmed fact, say you may be the Roleplayer. Near the vote lock, hang the best case available that Day. Remorseful voters: say 'insurance Innocent' on thin cases. A 'remorse' death proves the hanged player was Village. (seen in 80 games)
- A lone visitor to a Katana victim, confirmed by independent sources, is close to proof. An uncorroborated lone-visitor report is only its claimant's word, especially if it was held back or names the claimant's own accuser. Jesters and Yakuza invent exactly this pattern. (seen in 52 games)
- Yakuza: defending a doomed teammate usually backfires. Totals are checked against stated votes, so a hidden Innocent gets pinned on whoever defended. Do the vote math in night chat. Save them only if your votes really can; otherwise vote Guilty and say so. (seen in 67 games)
- Yakuza: send no daytime PMs to teammates, and none at all on a Day a teammate is under pressure. Everyone sees that a PM was sent, a Wiretap or Eavesdropper may read it, and PM triangles become the town's lead for the rest of the game. (seen in 74 games)

## Everyone
- Keep a notepad of every player's stated vote, plus the announced Yes counts and trial totals. A trial mismatch usually comes from a miscast, a lie or a Merciful/Zealot trait. Ask that player once to re-check their reminder (what they cast and how it counted) before you theorize. Only bring in a Fixer once a result shows one. With a Fixer shown, a trial of a Village claimant that comes in one Guilty above the stated votes usually means the Yakuza wanted that hang. Yes counts can't be altered, so a Yes mismatch is a lie. (seen in 24 games)
- Answer a direct question about your night action in one plain sentence the first time you're asked. Evasion repeated over Days becomes a case of its own. (seen in 29 games)
- An unexplained Katana survival can come from a Shaky holder (attacks of level None), the one-time Narrow Escape trait, innate Defense or a made-up Paranoid notice. Don't hang on a survival alone. Failures on several different targets usually point to one Shaky holder, so Yakuza should move the Katana. (seen in 3 games)
- A Turncoat reads Evil to checks, counts as Good in a census and wins only with the Yakuza. You spot one by behaviour: a Village claimant who keeps protecting the main suspect and voting to save them. Count them as a Yakuza vote in lock math. If you are the Turncoat, work out the Yakuza from public results and offer one of them concrete help in a short PM. (seen in 2 games)

## Village
- Post any result that bears on a vote (night, target, exact wording) in your first speech. Results held back until after a rival claims look retrofitted. If a Ritualist may be in the game, put the full log in your will before you claim. (seen in 46 games)
- On Day 1 and Day 2, don't lead the talk and don't hint at a power role. The most visible voice is usually the Night 1 kill, and half-hints are the worst of both. (seen in 43 games)
- Don't announce tonight's target in public or by PM, and don't say you'll act tonight. That tells blockers and the Katana where to go. (seen in 52 games)
- Write tonight's plan into your will, along with what each possible result would mean. If you die, the town can still use it. (seen in 56 games)
- Once a mechanical result shows a player is Yakuza, look next at whoever defended them, PM'd them or pushed No or delay at their nomination. A proven liar may be a Jester, and an Evil Sheriff read fits Chaos Neutrals, Notorious and framed players too. (seen in 55 games)
- Jester signs: they self-nominate, ask for the rope or push hard with no evidence. Leave them alive if you can. If you do hang one, agree on a small named Guilty list with a buffer vote, and keep confirmed power roles on Innocent. (seen in 29 games)
- Hunt Neutral killers on purpose by reading every cause of death. A Serial Killer, Werewolf or ignite cause proves that killer is alive, and a game that goes on after the Yakuza seem dead points to a win-blocking Neutral. But count down to the lock in effective votes: Yakuza plus fixed Innocents (the nominee's own vote, declared Merciful voters, a likely Turncoat). The Day before the lock is your last real hang, so give it to your best Yakuza lead and turn down 'one more night'. (seen in 21 games)

## Yakuza
- Agree in Night 1 chat on a real Village cover for each member, one that fits their tokens and real visits. Vanilla Villager, Grunt and Yakuza-only roles tend to get you hanged. Check every claim against the visit results already posted, and keep it: switching once cornered reads as a confession. (seen in 34 games)
- Every member should submit the agreed Katana holder, target and night action. With two of you left, one missing vote cancels the kill. (seen in 44 games)
- Never speak from your hidden perspective ('my Katana', facts only night chat told you). Slips like that have hanged Yakuza on their own. (seen in 14 games)
- Choose kills by future output: a Census Taker first, then group and count roles, then exact-role checkers when your covers are weak. A claimed shooter is usually guarded, so deal with the protector first. On Night 1, prefer a quiet but capable target over the Day 1 organizer, and send your most expendable member as holder. (seen in 40 games)
- After a failed attack, assume protection or innate Defense and remove the protector rather than retrying blindly. One retry on a later night can tell Narrow Escape from real Defense. (seen in 42 games)
- Don't flatly deny a visit that a count, group or watch could expose. Admit it at once with the cover you agreed in night chat. (seen in 20 games)

## Coroner
- On the first night such a body exists, examine one that would settle a live question: one side of a duplicate claim, a hanged suspect, or a victim whose will may be forged. Post night, target and exact role the next Day. Skip cleaned bodies and undisputed Katana victims, and never announce tomorrow's exam. (seen in 4 games)

## Medic
- Protect the likeliest Katana targets: vocal analysts early, claimed investigators later. Vary your pattern, and never visit a likely Veteran. The night after you claim Medic, protect yourself. Use Strong tokens once a Strong attacker is credible, because dying with tokens wastes them. (seen in 43 games)

## Vigilante
- Shoot on a lead, not on proof: a break in the target's own claim, votes or visits. Don't fire at a bare claim clash, at a claimed never-blocks-wins Neutral, or to 'test' an unverified claim. Dying with unused tokens is the usual failure: you can't use tokens once dead, so fire once a lead exists and before the Yakuza near parity. At parity, fire even on a coin flip. (seen in 34 games)
- Basic and Standard shots fail on a Serial Killer's or Arsonist's Standard Defense. Aim those at Yakuza suspects, and use Strong (or the hang) on a Neutral killer. If a shot kills, post the night, target and the role you learned. (seen in 29 games)
