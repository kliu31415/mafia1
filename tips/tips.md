# Tips from past games

Written 2026-10-16 12:12 UTC by opus-5.5 with `uv run tips.py build`, from the 405 active entries of tips_notebook.json (98 games). Players never see this preamble; they see every section below. Earlier sheets are in history/. Entries last checked against rules 8f2138dae76eb0b8.

## Most often decisive
- Check the mechanics before you call something a contradiction. Only the Mayor, Vulture, Hitman and Saint are Unique, so two claimants of any other role can both be real, and two real holders often pick the same targets. Still, 'roles repeat' is not evidence for either claimant. Test both that Day or night (a watch, an exact-role check, a target whose visits are known) and look for a detail a copier couldn't have. (seen in 50 games)
- Before you vote Guilty, name one corroborated mechanical break: a Yakuza-only role claim, a first-person slip about an action their claim can't take, a knowledge leak, or a corroborated lone visitor to a Katana victim. One unverified claimant's result, possibly Roleplayer noise, usually isn't enough. Near the vote lock, though, hang the best case you have rather than waiting. (seen in 78 games)
- A visit shows where someone went, not what they did. A report that X was the lone visitor to a Katana victim is near-proof only when a second source backs it up (a confirming track, visitors who confirm themselves). One unverified watch or map that was held back, or that names the reporter's own accuser, is just a claim. (seen in 52 games)
- Yakuza: don't defend a teammate who is doomed. Totals are compared with stated votes, so a hidden Innocent shows up and your defense is remembered later. Defend only if night-chat math shows your votes can actually save them. Otherwise vote with the expected majority and say how you voted. (seen in 66 games)
- Investigators: once a result bears on a vote, post the night, the target and the exact result. If you hold it back and reveal it after a rival claims, it reads as made up after the fact. With no result yet, don't name your exact role, and put your full log in your will before claiming in case a Ritualist is in the game. (seen in 45 games)

## Everyone
- Ballots are secret, so keep a notepad of each player's stated Yes/No and Guilty/Innocent beside the announced totals. A stated vote that doesn't fit a small trial is usually a miscast. Ask that player once to re-check their reminder, and don't hang on the mismatch alone. (seen in 22 games)
- When a total doesn't fit the stated votes, list the causes first: a miscast, a lie (often from a Neutral or Turncoat), or a Merciful or Zealot trait. Ask voters what they cast and how it counted. Suspect a Fixer only once something shows one exists. If one is shown, an extra Guilty on a Village claimant usually means the Yakuza wanted that hang. (seen in 11 games)
- One unexplained survival proves little. It can be innate Defense, the hidden Narrow Escape trait (saves any player once), or a Shaky holder whose Katana never kills. Failures on several different targets point to Shaky. Attack the same player again later rather than writing them off. (seen in 4 games)
- Before each night, write tonight's plan and what each possible result would mean into your will. Results from the night you die never reach it, so post important results while you're alive. (seen in 55 games)
- Answer a direct question about your night action in one plain sentence the first time it's asked. Dodging over several Days becomes a case against you in its own right. (seen in 29 games)

## Village
- Ask everyone to state their votes, then compare those with the totals. Once someone is shown Yakuza by mechanics, look at who argued to delay their hang or traded PMs with them. A lone Evil Sheriff read is not that kind of proof: Jesters and other Chaos Neutrals, Notorious and framed players all read Evil. (seen in 54 games)
- Don't announce tonight's target or that you'll use a token, in public or by PM. A Wiretap may read every PM, and announcing tells blockers and the Katana where to go. (seen in 52 games)
- On Day 1 and Day 2, don't hint at a power role. A half-hint marks you for the Night 1 Katana. If pressed, give one calm non-answer, or claim plainly once. (seen in 42 games)
- Watch for Jester signs: self-nominating, asking for the rope, voting Yes on their own nomination, a reckless claim that's easy to disprove. Leave them alive. If the table hangs one anyway, keep Guilty votes to a bare majority, with confirmed power roles voting Innocent, because the revenge kills a random Guilty voter. (seen in 28 games)
- Hunt win-blocking Neutral killers on purpose. Read every cause of death and keep a list of unclaimed or untested claimants. A Not Evil read doesn't clear them, and you can't hang one at two alive. But while two Yakuza may be alive at 5-6 players, the hang usually belongs to your best Yakuza lead. (seen in 21 games)
- Count down to the vote lock in effective votes: the Yakuza plus fixed Innocents (a declared Merciful voter, a likely Turncoat). The Day before the lock is your last real hang, so turn down 'one more night of checks' then. (seen in 15 games)
- A Turncoat reads Evil to checks but counts as Good in a census and wins only if the Yakuza win. A Village claimant who protects the main suspect every night, votes to save them and PMs them is likely one. Count them as a Yakuza vote in the lock math. (seen in 2 games)
- If you're Remorseful, a Guilty vote on a hanged Villager kills you. Vote Guilty only on corroborated cases, and say so before the vote. A 'remorse' death proves the hanged player was Village, so look again at who pushed that case.

## Yakuza
- Don't PM teammates during the Day. On any Day a teammate is under pressure, send no PMs at all. Do all planning in night chat, because the town sees every PM that's sent. (seen in 72 games)
- Every member should submit the agreed holder and target every night. With two Yakuza, one missing vote cancels the kill. (seen in 43 games)
- Fake-claim a role whose visits and tokens you can account for every night, and check it against every visit result already posted. Expect the real holder to counterclaim. Once a claim breaks, switching to another one is usually fatal. (seen in 34 games)
- Never claim a Yakuza-only role or Vanilla Villager (absent unless a list names it). Agree a Village cover for each member on Night 1. (seen in 27 games)
- Kill whoever's next result can narrow down your team: a Census Taker first, then counting and group roles, then exact-role checkers. A claimed shooter is the protectors' obvious ward. (seen in 39 games)

## Coroner
- Examine bodies that settle a live question, such as one side of a duplicate claim or a hanged suspect, on the first night you can. Post night, target and role in one message, and don't spend a token on cleaned bodies. (seen in 4 games)

## Medic
- Protect the likeliest Katana targets: vocal analysts early, claimed investigators later. Don't visit a possible Veteran. Spend a token on yourself the night after you claim, and switch to Strong protection once a Neutral killer shows up. (seen in 42 games)

## Vigilante
- Holding all your shots is the usual failure. Fire on a lead, meaning a break in the target's own claim, votes or visits. A claim clash, Day 1, an untested Medic claim or a never-blocks-wins Neutral is not a lead. Basic and Standard shots fail on a Serial Killer or Arsonist, so leave those to the hang. (seen in 34 games)
