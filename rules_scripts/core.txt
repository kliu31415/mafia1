=== PART 1: THE GAME ===

R1 (Overview and winning)
You are playing a Mafia-style game with around 10 players (depending on setup), each in a faction. An omniscient
moderator runs the game. The game alternates Day and Night phases, starting with Day 1. Day 1 is for talking only:
there are no nominations or trials, and it is shorter than later Days (R16).
- For most roles, you win iff your faction wins. A faction typically wins iff it has players alive at the end.
- The game ends as soon as all living players, ignoring roles that never block wins, belong to one faction. This is
  checked after every death; deaths that happen together at night are checked together.
- Some Neutral roles never block another faction's win (their role says so); their own wins don't depend on how
  the game ends. Every other Neutral blocks wins: while one is alive, the game goes on. So a game that hasn't ended
  doesn't show that a Yakuza is still alive.
- A trait can add a condition to your own win, take your faction's win from your teammates, or make you win with
  another faction (R24); only Turncoat changes when the game ends, and no trait changes which faction wins.

R2 (Factions)
- Village (Good): the largest faction, but with weaker roles and less information.
- Yakuza (Evil): fewer players, stronger roles; they know each other and share a night chat.
- Neutral: each Neutral player is their own one-person faction, even if two players have the same Neutral role.
The list of roles in the game is not public. A role may appear more than once in a game.

R15 (Alignments)
Every player has one of three alignments: Good (Village), Evil (Yakuza) or Neutral. Some roles and effects change
how a player's alignment appears; see R19.

R18 (Terms)
- Living players: players who haven't died or left. Every count in these rules ("majority", "random player")
  means living players unless it says otherwise.
- Majority: strictly more than half; exactly half is not a majority.
- Action: anything a role does at night by choice or by its rules (investigating, shooting, blocking, ...).
- Visit: an action on another player counts as A visiting B, unless it is an Astral Visit (R6).
- Astral Visit: an action that is not a visit. Abilities that react to visits (Tracker, Lookout,
  Fortune Teller) never see or react to it.
- Alignment check: a result that reports alignment (Sheriff, Seer). See R19 for what they see.
- Exact-role check: a result that reports or tests an exact role (Oracle, Ritualist, Mortician, Coroner, a Vigilante's kill).
- Token: a limited-use resource a role starts with. Spent tokens are gone; a role-blocked action spends none.

R11 (The circle)
Players sit in a circle. The dead (and players who leave) are removed from it.

=== PART 2: THE DAY ===

R4 (Day phase, chat, nominations and trials)
Chat:
- There is a public chat and private chats between exactly two players.
- Private messages are not secret: whenever A sends B a private message, everyone sees that A sent B a private
  message, but only A and B see its contents.
Nominations:
- Any player may publicly nominate someone, including themself. All chat stops at once.
- Everyone votes Yes/No at the same time; nobody sees another vote before casting their own. The nominator votes
  Yes by default. There are no abstentions: a player who doesn't vote counts as voting No. Votes are secret: once
  all votes are in, only the number of Yes votes is announced (the nominator is named), never who voted which way.
  Each voter is privately reminded of their own vote.
- If a majority of all votes counted are Yes, the nominee goes on trial.
Trials:
- The nominee says the first public message and may defend themself; others may chat publicly and privately.
  After everyone else has had a turn, the nominee gets one last public message before the vote.
- Then everyone votes Guilty/Innocent at the same time (no abstentions: a player who doesn't vote counts as voting
  Innocent). Votes are secret: only the Guilty and Innocent totals are announced, never who voted which way. Each
  voter is privately reminded of their own vote.
- If a majority of all votes counted are Guilty, the nominee is Executed by Hanging, a Divine Attack, and the Day
  ends immediately.
- If three nominations pass (failed ones don't count) and all three trials end Innocent, the Day ends.
Voting Yes or Guilty on yourself is allowed.

R16 (Day length)
Day chat goes around the circle in turns. Each Day the program picks a random living player to speak first;
turns then go around the circle from them in the same order every round. Besides the endings in R4, the Day ends when a full round passes in
which nobody sends a public message or nominates, except that anyone sent a private message during that round
first gets one last turn. The Day also ends after at most 8 rounds (2 on Day 1).

R22 (Speaking budget)
Day messages and nominations cost speaking budget. Each player earns budget every round of a Day, up to a cap, and
part of what they have left carries into the next Day (nothing carries over from Day 1). A message's price grows
with its length and, after the first rounds of a Day, with the round; private messages cost less than public ones.
A nomination costs budget too. On a trial turn prices are lower and each speaker also gets a trial-only budget,
spent first and lost when the turn ends; the accused's first defense message is free up to a set length. The
program sets the numbers for each game and tells every player at the start.

R13 (Last wills)
Players may write and update a last will at the start of each Night, when they choose their night action
(everyone is asked, even with no action), at the beginning of each Day from Day 2 on (except those who died the
night before) and during any of their own Day turns, including on Day 1. Results that arrive later in a
night can't be added that night. When a player dies, their last will is shared publicly at once.

R25 (Day actions)
Some roles act during the Day. A day action is taken on one of your own speaking turns, along with your messages;
not on a trial turn. It takes effect after a random delay: anywhere from right after that turn to n-1 speaking turns
later, n being the number of players alive when it was taken, every delay equally likely. Only Day speaking turns
count, not trial turns; a turn that starts a trial ends with that trial. So its timing doesn't show whose turn it
was. If the Day ends first, it takes effect as the Day ends. If you died since taking it, it doesn't take effect.
Day actions due at the same time take effect one at a time, in random order. A death it causes is announced at once
(R12), and the game ends if that leaves one faction (R1). A day action isn't a visit, and night protections don't
last into the Day.

=== PART 3: THE NIGHT ===

R5 (Night phase)
Chat is off for most players; some (usually a faction, like the Yakuza) have a private night chat. The moderator
wakes players one by one, or faction by faction, to act. When the Night ends, the moderator works out every
effect. By default, effects are announced publicly at the start of the next Day; some are not announced.

R7 (Optional actions)
Every ability is optional ("may") unless its role says "must": any player may choose not to act at Night, not to
use a Day ability, and not to spend a token. Effects a role or trait has automatically, with no choice (e.g. a
Sleepwalker's nightly visit, R24), aren't optional, but a role-block still stops them (R9).

R6 (Visits)
- When A performs an action on B, A visits B, unless the action is an Astral Visit.
- A role that targets a player may target itself unless its text says otherwise (e.g. "other than yourself").
- A player whose role can target others but who targets themself shows as visiting themself.
- A player whose action can only affect themself does not show as visiting anyone.
- An action that targets no player (e.g. a Veteran's alert) is not a visit.
- A player who visits the same target with several actions in one night is one visitor to that target: anything
  that lists, counts or reacts to visitors (a watch, a Veteran's alert) treats them once.

R9 (Role-blocks)
A role-blocked player's actions that night have no effect, exactly as if they had chosen not to act: no visit,
no tokens spent. It lasts only that one night (not the next Day or later nights). Role-blocked players are told
they were role-blocked. Any player can be role-blocked unless their role says otherwise; blocking a player who
has no night action, or who chose not to act, has no other effect, but they are still told. A role-blocker learns
only whether the block failed because its target can't be role-blocked. A role-blocker can't choose the same
player two nights in a row (a night it doesn't act breaks the run).
A result a role gets with no action at all (its text says "This needs no action") isn't an action, so a role-block
doesn't stop it; automatic actions, such as a Sleepwalker's visit or the Seer's random pair, are stopped (R7).

R10 (Simultaneous night resolution)
All night actions happen at the same time. Think of it as two steps: first everyone attempts their actions,
then all results happen together. So investigations that give their result at the end of the night (e.g. the
Seer's random pair) draw from and report on everyone alive when the night began, including players who die that
night.

R21 (Effects that outlast their user)
An effect set up at night for the following Day (e.g. a Prosecutor's prosecution) still happens if its user dies
that night, unless it needs its user to act during that Day; those lapse. Any token spent on it is gone either way
(R18).

R20 (Night resolution checklist): a summary of the rules above, in the order the moderator applies them.
1. Remove actions of role-blocked players (R9), automatic ones included (a Sleepwalker's visit, the Seer's pair).
   Party Animal, Pharmacist and Veteran can't be role-blocked.
2. Work out who visits whom (R6), including the Katana holder's visit (R8) and a Sleepwalker's random visit.
3. Attacks: each kills iff its level beats the target's Defense (R3). Defenses don't stack; the highest counts.
   A trap stops one attack before Defense and may hit its attacker back (Trapper).
4. Results for investigators, using the visits and appearances (R19).
5. Notifications: attackers learn whether they succeeded, attacked players learn the attacking role (R3, with the
   Katana exception in R8), protectors learn whether they stopped an attack (R17), role-blocked players are told
   (R9), Yakuza learn each other's actions (R8).
6. Deaths are revealed at the start of the Day (R12); the win check runs on all of the night's deaths together (R1).

=== PART 4: ATTACKS, DEATH AND INFORMATION ===

R3 (Attack and Defense)
- Levels, lowest to highest: None, Basic, Standard, Strong, Divine. Players have Defense None by default.
- An Attack kills iff its level is strictly higher than the target's Defense.
- Defenses don't stack: a player with several sources of Defense has the highest of them.
- By default the attacker learns whether the Attack succeeded (not the target's Defense), and the target learns
  the role that attacked them (not the attacker's name or Attack level). A target hit by several Attacks in one
  night learns the role behind each, including those that failed. The Katana is an exception (R8).

R17 (Protection feedback)
Protectors (e.g. the Medic) are told whether their protection stopped an attack, but not whose attack it was, and a
protected player is never told who protected them. A player who is attacked is still told as in R3. An attack of
level None (R24) counts as stopped by any protection on its target.

R12 (Death reveals)
When someone dies, their cause of death (e.g. Katana, Vigilante, Execution by Hanging) and last will are revealed
publicly; their role and alignment are not. If several attacks were each fatal, every fatal cause is revealed;
attacks the player survived aren't. The dead leave the circle and can't take part in the game any more. A player
whose win ends their part in the game (a hanged Jester, an Executioner who leaves) is the exception: the
announcement of their win names their role.

R14 (Stall rule)
If a Day begins and nobody has died in the three previous Nights and three previous Days, everyone loses. Deaths
from last Night count even though they're announced today. Every death counts; leaving (like the Executioner's)
doesn't.

R19 (What investigations see)
Alignment checks (Sheriff: Evil / Not Evil; Seer: compares Good / Neutral / Evil) see a player's true alignment,
except:
- Yakuza holding the Katana at the end of the night: Good (Sheriff: Not Evil), even if they died that night.
- Player framed by the Fabricator that night: Evil.
- A player with the Notorious or Turncoat trait (R24): Evil.
- Sheriff on Neutrals: Chaos roles read Evil, Killing roles Not Evil (each Neutral role is under one of these
  headings in Part 6).
  (Seers see every Neutral as Neutral.)
Exact-role checks see the true role, except that they see a Disguised player's disguise (R24).
Visit-based results (Tracker, Lookout, Fortune Teller) are never changed by any of these.


=== PART 5: THE YAKUZA ===

R8 (Yakuza and the Katana)
- One random Yakuza starts with the Katana. The Yakuza share a private chat at night. Each Yakuza chat message
  is at most 200 words; longer messages are cut.
- Each night the Yakuza vote, at the same time, on who holds the Katana that night (and the next Day) and whom it
  attacks, if anyone. Both choices need a majority of living Yakuza; with two Yakuza alive, both must agree.
  Without a majority on a target, no attack happens; without a majority on a holder, the Katana doesn't move.
  A majority may move the Katana without attacking. That is the Katana's one attack a night; traits (R24) can
  add more, and a Village trait makes an attack that looks just like one, so several players may be attacked
  with the Katana in one night, and not every Katana attack is the Yakuza's.
- The Katana attack is Standard (a trait can change its level, R24) and is dealt by whoever holds it at the end
  of the night. It is an action and a
  visit by that player (the new holder, if it moved that night): role-blocking them stops it, and Trackers see
  them visit the target. Moving or passing the Katana isn't an action: a role-block doesn't stop it (a blocked
  new holder still gets the Katana, but their attack fails), and it isn't a visit.
- The holder may also use their own role's night action.
- The holder appears Good to alignment checks that night (R19).
- The attacked player only learns they were attacked with the Katana, not the Yakuza role.
- At the end of the night, each Yakuza learns what actions the other Yakuza chose, but not their results
  (e.g. whether the Pharmacist's block worked) or whether a teammate was role-blocked.
- Role-blocked Yakuza still vote on the Katana's holder and target; the votes aren't actions.
- If the holder dies, the Katana passes to a random living Yakuza, if any; a holder who dies at night passes it on
  after that night's results. The living Yakuza are told who holds it now (and so learn that the holder died).

=== PART 6: ROLES ===

Every role lists the same fields. Defaults, unless a role says otherwise: Defense None, no tokens, appears as its
true alignment (R19), night action is a visit.

--- Village (Good) ---

Townsfolk (most villagers, helpful to the Village)

Vanilla Villager
  Night: none.

Investigative

Sheriff
  Night: may investigate one player. Learn Evil or Not Evil (alignment check, R19).

Tracker
  Night: may track one player. Learn whom they visited.

Lookout
  Night: may watch one player. Learn who visited them, not counting your own visit.

Seer
  Tokens: 1 seer token.
  Night: at the end of every night, you are told two random players other than yourself who were alive when the
  night began (players who die that night count, R10), and whether their alignments (Good/Neutral/Evil) are the
  same (alignment check, R19). Instead, you may spend a token to choose the
  two living players yourself (not including yourself). A chosen pair is a visit to both players; the random
  pair isn't a visit. A role-block stops both. If fewer than two other players were alive when the night began,
  there is no random pair: you are told so, and learn nothing.

Fortune Teller
  Night: at the end of every night, you learn two numbers about that night, each labeled: how many players were
  visited (a player visited several times counts once), and how many players visited anyone (a player who visited
  several players counts once). This needs no action. Self-visits (R6) count in both numbers.
  Example: A holds the Katana, attacks B and also uses their own role's action on C, D visits B, and E protects
  themself. Visited: B, C and E, so 3. Visitors: A, D and E, so 3. Equal numbers don't mean nobody was visited
  twice.

Oracle
  Night: may guess one player's exact role (Astral Visit). At the end of the night, learn whether you were right.

Coroner
  Tokens: 3 coroner tokens.
  Night: may spend a token to examine one player who died before the night began (not one who left the game, and not
  one who dies tonight). You learn their role, unless a Mortician cleaned them: then you learn only that. This is an
  Astral Visit (R6) and an exact-role check (R19).

Auditor
  Night: may audit one player other than yourself. You learn every vote they cast during the Day just before this
  Night, in order: Yes or No on each nomination and Guilty or Innocent in each trial. If they cast none, you learn
  that.

Insomniac
  Tokens: 2 insomniac tokens.
  Night: may spend a token to stay awake. At the end of the night, you learn the roles of up to 3 players who visited
  someone that night (R6; a visit to themselves counts), but not who they are: if more than 3 players visited, 3 of
  them are drawn at random. The roles are listed in alphabetical order, and a role shows twice if two of those
  players have it. If fewer than 3 players visited, you learn all of their roles, and if nobody visited, you learn
  that. Players who visited and then died that night count. Only visits count: an action that isn't a visit (e.g.
  an Astral Visit or a Veteran's alert) doesn't. Staying awake is not a visit. This is an exact-role check (R19).

Protective

Medic
  Tokens: 2 medic tokens.
  Night: may protect one player other than yourself, giving them Standard Defense. Spend 1 token to give Strong
  Defense instead, and 1 token to protect yourself instead of another player; the two can be combined (so
  protecting yourself costs 1 token with Standard Defense and 2 with Strong).

Trapper
  Night: may place a trap on one player other than yourself and other than the player your trap is already on, or
  may remove your trap (removing it isn't a visit). You have one trap at a time; a new trap replaces the old one at
  once. Traps last even after you die. A trap is armed from the next night on: the first time anyone makes a Basic,
  Standard or Strong Attack on the trapped player on a later night, the trap is used up and stops that attack
  (chosen at random if there are several; Divine Attacks ignore it); any other attacks resolve as usual. Several
  traps on one player act one at a time, in random order, each stopping a different attack. The trap acts before
  Defense, after any protection that acts before all others. If the stopped attack was a visit, its attacker is
  dealt a Strong Attack, whatever their faction; an Astral attack is stopped without one. You learn that your trap
  went off, and nothing else (not whose attack, nor whether the trap killed).

Killing

Vigilante
  Tokens: 3 vigilante tokens.
  Night: may shoot one player: 1 token for a Basic Attack, 2 for Standard, 3 for Strong. If your shot kills them,
  you also learn their role (exact-role check, R19).

Veteran
  Tokens: 3 alert tokens.
  You can't be role-blocked.
  Night: may spend a token to go on alert (not a visit, R6, so nothing that reacts to visits counts it). While on
  alert you have Standard Defense and you deal a Strong Attack to every player who visits you that night, whatever
  their faction (a role-blocker who visits you is attacked too), however many there are. Astral Visits aren't
  visits, so they aren't attacked. At the end of the night you learn whether anyone visited you and, if so, how
  many, and whether your attack on each of them succeeded, but nothing about who they were.

Support

Prosecutor
  Tokens: 2 prosecutor tokens.
  Night: may spend a token to prosecute someone. At the start of the next Day, before anyone speaks, the moderator
  announces that someone is being prosecuted; this counts as a successful nomination and they go to trial. It
  counts toward the Day's three-nomination limit, and an execution ends the Day as usual. If the trial needs a
  "nominator", the moderator picks a random player.

Party Animal
  Immune to role-blocks.
  Night: may visit one player, role-blocking them.

--- Yakuza (Evil) ---
All Yakuza share the Katana and night chat (R8).

Grunt
  Night: none of its own.

Pharmacist
  Immune to role-blocks.
  Night: may role-block one player.

Ritualist
  Tokens: 2 ritual tokens.
  Night: may guess one player's exact role (Astral Visit), spending 1 token for a Standard Attack or 2 for a
  Divine Attack. If right, the Attack is dealt. If wrong, the moderator publicly announces at the start of the
  next Day that you are a Ritualist. The tokens are spent either way.

Fabricator
  Tokens: 1 fabricator token.
  Night: may visit one non-Yakuza player to frame them (no token needed): that night they appear Evil to
  alignment checks (R19); other results are unchanged. On the same visit, you may spend a token to forge that
  player's last will (not anyone else's): if they die of any cause that night, your forged will replaces theirs.
  Framing and forging together are one visit.

Mortician
  Tokens: 3 mortician tokens.
  Night: may spend a token to try to clean someone. If they die that night by any cause, they are cleaned: their
  death is revealed publicly with its cause, but their last will shows as "cleaned by mortician". You are
  privately told their role and last will. A Coroner who examines a cleaned player learns nothing. The token is
  spent even if they survive.

Informant
  You passively see how every player voted in each Guilty/Innocent trial vote, right after the vote, as each vote
  counted (after any change such as a Fixer's or Merciful's, so a vote may differ from what its voter says). This
  is not a night action or a visit, so role-blocks don't stop it. Nobody is told that an Informant exists, and the
  other Yakuza aren't told the votes: what you share in the night chat is up to you.

--- Neutral ---
Each Neutral player is their own faction (R2).

Killing (Neutral roles that kill; Not Evil to the Sheriff, R19)

Serial Killer
  Defense: Standard.
  Win: only alone (not even with another Serial Killer).
  Night: may deal a Strong Attack to one player.

Arsonist
  Defense: Standard.
  Win: only alone (not even with another Arsonist).
  Night: may douse one player, or ignite (Astral Visit), dealing a Strong Attack to every doused player. A doused
  player who isn't Good (Yakuza or Neutral) is told at the end of the night that an Arsonist doused them; Good
  players are never told. Igniting uses up every douse: players who survive it are no longer doused.

Chaos (Neutral roles that win by other means; Evil to the Sheriff, R19)

Jester
  Defense: Basic.
  Never blocks wins (R1).
  Win: iff you are hanged during a Day (other daytime deaths don't count). You lose if the game ends with you
  alive.
  Revenge: if you are hanged, the following night a random Guilty voter from your trial who was alive when that
  night began is dealt a Divine Attack (Astral Visit). If nobody voted Guilty, nothing happens.

Executioner
  Defense: Basic.
  Never blocks wins (R1).
  At the start you get a random target: any player except yourself.
  Win: iff your target is hanged during a Day while you are alive (other daytime deaths don't count). You lose if
  the game ends with your target alive.
  On winning: the moderator reveals you at once and you leave the game. Leaving works exactly like dying (you
  leave the circle for good) except it doesn't count as a death. The following night, a random Guilty voter from
  the trial who was alive when that night began (not you, since you left) is dealt a Divine Attack (Astral Visit). If nobody voted
  Guilty, nothing happens.
  If your target dies any other way, or leaves the game, you become a Jester.

=== PART 7: TRAITS ===

R24 (Traits)
Some players also have a trait: an extra ability on top of their role. You are told your own trait at the start
(except a hidden trait, which says so); nobody else is told, not even your teammates, and whether a game has any
traits is secret. Investigations never see traits: they report roles and alignments only. A trait stays with you if
your role changes. A player may have more than one trait, but never the same one twice. Each trait below says who can get it (Who), whether it is
hidden from its holder (Hidden), whether at most one player in a game has it (Unique), what it does (Effect) and
which traits nobody holds together with it (Never with). A trait's night action is used in addition to your role's
own, and a role-block stops it too.

--- Village players only ---

Martyr
  Who: Village players only.
  Effect: If you are hanged, the announcement of your death also says you were a Martyr and names every player whose
  vote counted as Guilty in your trial. Your role stays hidden. A Martyr who dies any other way shows nothing.

Cursed
  Who: Village players only.
  Effect: If you die, a random living Good player (if any) is dealt a Divine Attack at the end of the night: the
  night you died, or the night after the Day you died. It comes after all of that night's other actions and attacks,
  so the victim is drawn from the Good players still alive then, and it counts as a death that night. Its cause is
  "a curse". It needs no action, so a role-block doesn't stop it.

Avenger
  Who: Village players only.
  Effect: If you are killed at night, every player whose attack would have killed you is dealt a Basic Attack (an
  Astral Visit) that same night. Defense and protection apply as usual (R3); a kill's cause is "Avenger". Attacks
  that weren't fatal on their own, and deaths by Day, set nothing off. It needs no action, so a role-block doesn't
  stop it.

Kindred
  Who: Village players only.
  Effect: After each trial vote you cast, you privately learn up to two random other players whose votes counted the
  same way as the vote you cast (if fewer did, you learn that). It needs no action, so a role-block doesn't stop it.

Remorseful
  Who: Village players only.
  Effect: If you vote Guilty in a trial and that player is hanged and was Village (their true alignment), you die at
  once; the cause is "remorse". It goes by the vote you cast. It isn't an attack, so nothing that reacts to attacks
  counts it.
  Never with: Zealot.

Ashamed
  Who: Village players only.
  Effect: If you vote Innocent in a trial and that player is hanged and wasn't Village (their true alignment: Yakuza
  or Neutral), you die at once; the cause is "shame". It goes by the vote you cast. It isn't an attack, so nothing
  that reacts to attacks counts it.
  Never with: Merciful or Turncoat.

Faultline
  Who: Village players only.
  Effect: After each trial vote you cast, you privately learn one random other player whose vote counted as Guilty
  and one whose vote counted as Innocent (if all the others voted one way, you learn that). It needs no action, so a
  role-block doesn't stop it.

Notorious
  Who: Village players only.
  Effect: Alignment checks see you as Evil (R19).
  Never with: Turncoat.

Stolen Blade
  Who: Village players only.
  Effect: Once per game, at night, you may attack another living player with a stolen Katana: a Standard Attack,
  besides your role's own action. It is an action and a visit by you, and it looks exactly like the Yakuza's Katana
  attack: a kill's cause is "Katana", and a surviving target learns only that they were attacked with the Katana.
  You learn only whether it killed. A role-block stops it, and then it isn't used up.
  Never with: Armed.

Paranoid
  Who: Village players only.
  Hidden: you aren't told you have this trait.
  Effect: On each night, with probability 1/4, you are told you were attacked and survived, though nobody attacked
  you. The made-up attack is the Katana's 2/3 of the time, and otherwise a Vigilante's or a Serial Killer's (always
  the Katana's when neither role can be in the game); it is worded like a real one. Nothing else happens: it isn't a
  visit or an attack, and nobody else learns of it.
  Never with: Oblivious.

Turncoat
  Who: Village players only.
  Unique: at most one player in a game has it.
  Effect: You win only if the Yakuza win (alive or dead), and you lose otherwise, even if the Village wins.
  Alignment checks see you as Evil (R19). You stay Village in every other way: you aren't in the Yakuza's chat or Katana votes, you don't know who
  the Yakuza are, and they aren't told about you. The game's end ignores you, like a role that never blocks wins
  (R1): it ends when the other living players are all Village or all Yakuza.
  Never with: Ashamed, Notorious, Rival, Selfish, Selfless or Spiteful.

Spiteful
  Who: Village players only.
  Unique: at most one player in a game has it.
  Effect: If you are hanged, from then on you win only if the Yakuza win (alive or dead), and you lose otherwise,
  even if the Village wins. Until then you win with the Village as usual, and dying any other way changes nothing.
  Nobody is told you switched, and your will is revealed as usual.
  Never with: Rival, Selfish, Selfless or Turncoat.

--- Yakuza players only ---

Spare Blade
  Who: Yakuza players only.
  Effect: Once per game, at night, you may make one extra Katana attack on another living player, whoever holds the
  Katana and besides its own attack (which the Yakuza still choose as usual, R8). It is Standard, an action and a
  visit by you, and its target learns only that they were attacked with the Katana. A role-block stops it, and then
  it isn't used up.
  Never with: Armed or Berserker.

Usurper
  Who: Yakuza players only.
  Unique: at most one player in a game has it.
  Effect: You win only if the Yakuza win while you are the only Yakuza alive, and then you take the win alone: the
  other Yakuza, alive or dead, don't win. If the Yakuza win with another Yakuza alive, they win and you don't. Your
  teammates aren't told.
  Never with: Selfish or Selfless.

Shaky
  Who: Yakuza players only.
  Hidden: you aren't told you have this trait.
  Effect: Every Katana attack you make (the Katana's own while you hold it, or a Spare Blade attack) is level None,
  so it never kills (R3); it is still an attack and a visit.
  Never with: Berserker, Katana Expert or Katana Master.

Katana Expert
  Who: Yakuza players only.
  Effect: Once per game, on a night you hold the Katana, you may make its own attack Strong instead of Standard.
  Choose it with your night action and name the player the Katana attacks; it works only if you hold the Katana at
  the end of that night and it attacks that player, and only then is it used up. A role-block on you stops it along
  with the attack, and it isn't used up.
  Never with: Berserker, Katana Master or Shaky.

Katana Master
  Who: Yakuza players only.
  Effect: Like Katana Expert, but the attack is Divine, which no Defense, protection or trap stops.
  Never with: Berserker, Katana Expert or Shaky.

Berserker
  Who: Yakuza players only.
  Effect: Once per game, on a night you hold the Katana, you may make its attack rampage: besides its own attack, it
  makes a Standard Katana attack on every other player who visits its target that night, whatever their faction.
  These attacks on visitors aren't visits, and Astral Visits aren't visits, so their makers aren't attacked. Choose it
  with your night action and name the player the Katana attacks; it works only if you hold the Katana at the end of
  that night and it attacks that player, and only then is it used up. A role-block on you stops it along with the
  attack, and it isn't used up. Each attacked player learns only that they were attacked with the Katana; you learn
  how many players the rampage attacked and how many of those attacks killed, not who the visitors were.
  Never with: Katana Expert, Katana Master, Shaky or Spare Blade.

--- Village and Yakuza players only ---

Armed
  Who: Village and Yakuza players only.
  Effect: Once per game, at night, you may fire a hidden gun at another living player: a Basic Attack. It is an
  action and a visit, and a role-blocked shot isn't used up. You learn only whether it killed its target. A kill's
  cause is "a hidden gun".
  Never with: Spare Blade or Stolen Blade.

Bonded
  Who: Village and Yakuza players only.
  Effect: You and one other random Village or Yakuza player are bonded, and each of you is told the other's name
  (not their role or faction). If either of you dies, the other dies of heartbreak at once (its cause is
  "heartbreak"). Heartbreak isn't an attack, so nothing that reacts to attacks counts it.
  Never with: Rival.

Rival
  Who: Village and Yakuza players only.
  Effect: You and one other random player of your own faction are rivals, and each of you is told the other's name
  (not their role). Each of you wins only if your faction wins and the other has died by the end; whether you are
  alive doesn't matter. Your faction's win doesn't change.
  Never with: Bonded, Spiteful or Turncoat.

Selfish
  Who: Village and Yakuza players only.
  Effect: You win only if your faction wins and you are alive at the end. Your faction's win doesn't change; you
  just don't share it if you died.
  Never with: Selfless, Spiteful, Turncoat or Usurper.

Selfless
  Who: Village and Yakuza players only.
  Effect: You win only if your faction wins and you are dead at the end. Your faction's win doesn't change; you just
  don't share it if you're alive.
  Never with: Selfish, Spiteful, Turncoat or Usurper.

--- Any player ---

Grudge
  Who: Any player.
  Effect: If you are hanged, the next night one random player whose vote counted as Guilty in your trial, among
  those alive when that night begins, is dealt a Standard Attack (an Astral Visit). Defense and protection apply as
  usual. A kill's cause is "a grudge". It needs no action, so a role-block doesn't stop it.

Loud
  Who: Any player.
  Effect: You earn 1.5 times the usual speaking budget each round, and your cap is 1.5 times the usual (R22). Your
  prices and trial-only budget are the usual ones.

Curt
  Who: Any player.
  Effect: The length part of your message prices (R22) is doubled, so long messages cost you much more; each
  message's base fee, nominations and your trial-only budget are the usual ones.

Confidant
  Who: Any player.
  Effect: Once per game, on one of your own Day turns, you may send one private message hidden: nobody else is told
  it was sent, and the public ledger doesn't count it. Its recipient sees that it was hidden.

Zealot
  Who: Any player whose role doesn't change how much their own trial vote counts.
  Effect: Every Guilty/Innocent trial vote you cast counts as Guilty, whatever you vote, including in your own
  trial. Your nomination (Yes/No) votes are unchanged. Your private reminder shows both the vote you cast and how it
  counted, and everything that reports how you voted (e.g. the Auditor, a Martyr's announcement) uses the vote as
  counted.
  Never with: Merciful or Remorseful.

Merciful
  Who: Any player whose role doesn't change how much their own trial vote counts.
  Effect: Like Zealot, but your trial votes always count as Innocent.
  Never with: Ashamed or Zealot.

Drowsy
  Who: Any player whose role has a night action and can be role-blocked.
  Hidden: you aren't told you have this trait.
  Effect: On each night, with probability 1/2, you are role-blocked (R9) with no blocker. On those nights you are
  told you were role-blocked, as with any role-block.

Stocked
  Who: Any player whose role starts with tokens.
  Effect: You start with one more of each kind of token your role starts with.

Disguised
  Who: Any player.
  Effect: You are told a Village role (never your own), drawn at the start. Every exact-role check (R18) sees you as
  that role instead of yours, alive or dead; alignment checks still see your true alignment.

Narrow Escape
  Who: Any player.
  Hidden: you aren't told you have this trait.
  Effect: Once per game, on the first night an attack that isn't Divine would kill you (after Defense, protection
  and traps), you survive: every non-Divine attack on you that night fails instead. It works like Strong
  Defense on every night until then, so a night when nothing would have killed you (e.g. a Medic stopped the attack)
  doesn't use it up. Divine Attacks and deaths by Day aren't stopped. You are told about the failed attacks as for
  any others (R3).

Oblivious
  Who: Any player.
  Hidden: you aren't told you have this trait.
  Effect: You are never told about an attack you survive.
  Never with: Paranoid.

Sleepwalker
  Who: Any player.
  Effect: Every night you visit one random other living player, besides any action of your own; code draws it, and
  you aren't told whom. It does nothing but visit: anything that sees or reacts to visits (R6), such as a Tracker, a
  Lookout or a Veteran's alert, counts it. It isn't optional, but a role-block stops it (R7).
